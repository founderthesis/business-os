### Ishan Talathi

The cloud has four layers. At the bottom is the data centre, a building with power, cooling and network. Cloud providers rent you compute. Neoclouds rent you GPU servers and nothing else. Hyperscalers like AWS and Azure add a rich software layer on top. Ishan Talathi (https://www.linkedin.com/in/ishantalathi/ - @Ishan) is the founder of Leapswitch Networks, whose CloudPe platform is a full-stack cloud for everyday apps and AI (200 racks capacity). You may think a ₹100 Cr+ cloud company has no right to win against AWS. Ishan's answer: hyperscalers carry huge margins, spend heavily on products and marketing, and keep your data locked in. That's how an A100 GPU can cost $14 an hour there against about $3 on a neocloud. Despite the lower pricing, Leapswitch earns 50-60% margins. He started in 2006 as an engineering student reselling US servers to customers from online forums, and merged with an enterprise hosting business in 2024. The bet now is AI: everything they have is sold out, so the limit is the money to buy GPUs, and debt for that is hard to get in India.

### Sparsh Gupta

Getting visitors to a website gets costlier every year, because every business bids for the same three ad slots on Google. So the smarter bet is to convert more of the people you already pay for, and the tool for that is A/B testing, borrowed from drug trials: show half your visitors one headline and half another, and let statistics say which one sells more. In 2009 it barely existed outside eBay, Walmart and Booking.com, which had data science teams for it. Sparsh Gupta (https://www.linkedin.com/in/sparshgupta/ - @Sparsh) is the founder of Wingify, whose product VWO turned that into software anyone could use. The customer pastes one line of JavaScript on their site. When a page loads, it hides the headline, fetches the new version from Wingify's servers and swaps it in within milliseconds, while the rest of the page is still served from the customer's own server. Sparsh and his college friend Paras Chopra built it as a side project in 2009. Once they started charging, the first three months went from about $4K to $8K to $16K, and they knew they had PMF. They stayed bootstrapped for 15 years, until Everstone bought a majority stake in 2025. Today it's about $100M ARR with 800 people in 14 offices, and this year it merged with France's AB Tasty.

### Vishal Nigam

Most of India works out at a neighbourhood gym run by a single owner, often on paper registers, and plenty of those owners give up within a year. Vishal Nigam (https://www.linkedin.com/in/vishalnigamwtfgyms/ - @Vishal) is the founder of WTF Gyms, which is building the OYO++ for these gyms. Today it runs 264 gyms across three tiers: Powered, where WTF only manages a gym for a monthly fee, without its name on the door; WTF-branded gyms it takes over and runs; and WTF Black, the premium tier (think OYO Townhouse). In the long term, the gym is really just the distribution. Screens inside the gyms make up an ad network that brings in about a fifth of revenue, alongside its own supplements brand and a GLP-1 weight-loss programme.

### Suraj Dodeja

Moglix has raised about $470M to take industrial buying online, and yet most factory purchasing in India still happens offline, through distributors with local reach. Suraj Dodeja (https://in.linkedin.com/in/suraj-dodeja-a0a57724 - @Suraj) is coming at the same problem from the other end. ### Ankur Agrawal

When Ankur Agrawal (https://in.linkedin.com/in/ankuragrawalrioai - @Ankur) told me that Rio is a pharma quick comm business, I spent a lot of time pushing back: why wouldn't a Blinkit rule this market too, given they already have density and distribution? By the end of our conversation Ankur had changed my mind. You see, the consumer behaviour quick commerce runs on is totally different from how pharma retail works. For one, the consumer is not brand agnostic. They don't search for oat biscuits, they search for the specific brand their doctor prescribed. That means the basic quick commerce Pareto rule (20% of the assortment covers 80% of the demand) breaks. The business breaks if you don't stock the long tail. So Rio, delivering in 20 to 30 minutes across Delhi NCR, is built around depth. Each ~1,000 sq ft dark store targets 20,000+ medicines, against 5,000 to 7,000 at a chain like Apollo, and Rio reads prescriptions to build profiles of the doctors around each store prescribe, down to which brands they prescribe, then stock that. The key to success in this business - fill the whole prescription and people come back. Before Rio, Ankur built Tea Halt to 50+ takeaway outlets and then retrofitted smart fridges for retail at Gobly. Rio is at about 1,000 orders a day and Rs 2 Cr a month, has raised $6.5M led by Version One Ventures, and goes from three dark stores to 15 by around March 27.

## Manjunath Jyothinagara

India barely has advanced manufacturing capability, and three things make it hard: the process happens at nanometer scale, it runs on gases like arsine and phosphine that kill a person, and it is a deeply complex process (about 1,000 interdependent steps to turn a wafer into a chip, where a single bad step wastes everything done before it). Manjunath Jyothinagara (https://www.linkedin.com/in/mj-manjunath-jyothinagara/ - @{WhatsApp handle}) is the founder of KAS Group, which is building the advanced manufacturing ecosystem India doesn't have. He started with the high-precision systems that carry those poisonous gases from storage into the process tools and today the group builds everything around the machines in a fab, and increasingly the machines themselves. They crossed ₹300 crore revenue this year, all bootstrapped and cashflow positive from day one, and the 1000cr mark is not too far off with all the advanced manufacturing investment happening in India.

## Rohit Manglik

Many of you would have read about Meta paying $14B for a stake in Scale AI. Rohit Manglik (https://www.linkedin.com/in/rohitmanglik/ - @{WhatsApp handle}) is building that business out of India. He is the founder of InfoBay.AI, which sells pre-training and post-training data to frontier AI labs. Pre-training data is what these systems learn from, essentially the entire internet and a lot more, cleaned up and made machine readable. InfoBay did ₹100 Cr in FY26 at very healthy margins, with 400 people across five countries including a 44-strong AI research team, and the plan is to hit 1000 cr in the next 3 years (their customers currently have to wait 5 months for order fulfillment - that’s the tremendous amount of demand they are facing)

## Hari Kiran Chereddi

Hari Kiran Chereddi (https://www.linkedin.com/in/harichereddi/ - @{WhatsApp handle}) is the founder of HRV Pharma, an asset light bulk drugs pharma company doing nearly ₹500 Cr of revenue with a team of just 55. The easiest way to understand the business is this: the API is the actual ingredient inside a pill that does the work, and to sell it into markets like the US or Japan you need what is effectively a regulatory passport proving how it is made and tested. Traditionally, API companies build the factory and then get this passport tied to that plant. Hari realised the passport is the truly valuable asset and went out acquiring passports to sell various drugs in various markets - thereby becoming a supply aggregator platform for buyers. They earn their margin not by squeezing manufacturers, but by discovering inefficiencies in the market, and creating a supply chain running across multiple factories instead of being a single factory business. Unlike other businesses of this scale, they don’t have an IPO in the works (no external capital raised till date, cash rich with negative working capital!).

## Karmendra Kohli

Karmendra Kohli (https://www.linkedin.com/in/karmendra-kohli-86a7776/ - @{WhatsApp handle}) is the founder of SecurEyes, a cybersecurity advisory and assurance firm. SecurEyes plays in that third layer, telling boards and regulators where their risk actually is, then proving it by hacking in themselves. The first funding round is now on the table.

## Gautam Rege

Gautam Rege (https://www.linkedin.com/in/gautamrege/ - @{WhatsApp handle}) is the founder of Josh Software, a Pune engineering firm that builds the software sitting between your banking app and the bank's core system. Every balance check, transfer and KYC verification has to travel down to a legacy core banking platform like Oracle's Flexcube, which most banks can neither live with nor afford to rip out. Josh builds a configuration layer alongside it that absorbs most of the transaction checks and API calls, so the core does less work and the bank can ship new features without touching it. They hand over working source code instead of selling a product, so the IP stays with the bank. HDFC, Bandhan Bank and NPCI are all customers. The team is around 350 engineers, and roughly 40% of the work now comes from outside India. Their current push is Lingo, voice banking in Indian languages running entirely on open-weight models hosted inside the bank's own infrastructure.

## Alekh Sanghera

Alekh Sanghera (@{WhatsApp handle}, https://www.linkedin.com/in/alekh-sanghera-58a36778/) is the founder of FarMart - think of it as a Rapido kind of platform, but for agri-commodities procurement. Just as Rapido charges drivers a service fee rather than skimming a cut of every ride, FarMart makes money on the services layered around each transaction - offtake fees, logistics, finance, and platform access - rather than trading the spread. The model is brutal in its simplicity: zero inventory, seven-day delivery TAT, 10,000+ village-level procurement agents, and 80+ enterprise buyers including Britannia, Reliance Retail, and Heineken. At $350M in revenue with just 320 people, FarMart has only one peer at scale in India - ITC. A Punjab-rooted, World Bank-trained consultant, Alekh spent five years in painful pivots before cracking the model. Backed by ~$75M from General Catalyst, Matrix and Z47 - with an IPO in sight.

## Amit Koshal

Amit Koshal (@{WhatsApp handle}) is a serial entrepreneur and the cofounder of Twid. Amit was building the txtWeb product of Intuit when he decided to start Fashalot which was an offline to online play in the fashion retail space. And while that idea did not scale, it led them to discover the bigger opportunity in solving the complexity of earned reward/loyalty points. Let me explain the opportunity with the example of Flipkart’s Supercoins. Imagine the power of Flipkart’s Supercoins if you can spend them like cash when buying your next pair of jeans in a Levis store. This is what Twid enables. On the one hand, it integrates with retailers and allows them to accept reward points as a mode of payment, and on the other hand it integrates with issuers of rewards points and allows them to make the reward points fungible and acceptable as a mode of payment. For the customer - it’s a seamless experience at the time of checkout - Twid uses their phone number to calculate the reward points they have earned across apps and allows them to redeem those points instantly across multiple retailers. Twid has raised ~$17mn till date, and is responsible for powering 8% of the GMV for its merchant base as on date.

## Sundeep Mohindru

And last but not least - Sundeep Mohindru (@{WhatsApp handle}) is building an infrastructure layer for India that is as ambitious as the UPI. Just as UPI has removed friction around digital payments for consumers, TReDS is removing friction around business to business payments. The TReDS platform allows vendors to get paid for invoice instantly (through factoring and invoice discounting), allows banks to invest in debt of highly rated companies, and lets companies automate their accounts payable function. Sundeep is the founder of M1Xchange which today is responsible for 33% of the TReDS transactions in India despite competing with platforms run by large legacy players like Axis Bank & SIDBI. Sundeep is also the founder of Mynd Solutions which handles accounting and HR processes for customers both through technology products and managed services. The combined turnover of these companies is currently ~550cr, and Sundeep estimates that M1Xchange on its own will hit a 500 cr turnover within the next 3 years. M1Xchange has raised ~$24mn from Amazon, Beenext, Indiamart, SIDBI etc.

## Mayank Kumar

Mayank Kumar (https://www.linkedin.com/in/mayank-kumar-5573243/ - @{WhatsApp handle}) hardly needs an introduction. He co-founded upGrad with Ronnie Screwvala and, over a decade, helped steer it through multiple fundraises, international expansion and a string of acquisitions. upGrad has now crossed ₹2,000 Cr in revenue and is working towards a 2027 IPO. In 2024, Mayank started BorderPlus, a skilling-first mobility company that prepares Indian nurses, to begin with, for jobs in countries facing acute shortages. Unlike the traditional migration model, where multiple small agents hand talent from one to another, BorderPlus owns the entire journey from sourcing and skilling to migration and post-landing integration. His vision is to build something akin to an IIT for global careers: just as getting into an IIT can be a ticket to a well-paying job, getting into BorderPlus should become a ticket to a well-paying job abroad. With ageing populations across the developed world and the Indian government promoting global mobility, the sector has massive tailwinds. BorderPlus has raised about $7M and is on track to place 1,000+ nurses across Germany and Japan, with each placement generating ₹8-20 lakh in revenue.

## Vineet Agarwal

Vineet Agarwal (https://www.linkedin.com/in/vineetagarwaltci/ - @{WhatsApp handle}) is the MD of Transport Corporation of India, the listed logistics company his grandfather started in 1958 with one hired truck. TCI is now touching ₹5,000 Cr of revenue with ₹460 Cr of profit and a market cap of about ₹6,700 Cr. Freight still makes up roughly half the business, run largely on hired capacity with TCI owning just 1,500 of the 10,000+ trucks it operates. Supply chain contributes another ₹1,900 Cr, including 17 million sq ft of warehousing, automotive milk runs, e-commerce fulfilment and quick-commerce replenishment. Rail, run through a JV with CONCOR, has become increasingly important post-GST, moving cars in bulk from factories to yards near dealers and cutting delivery times sharply. The big growth bet now is Seaways: six coastal ships today, two more arriving this year, in a business that is still under 15% of revenue but contributes over 40% of gross profits. Coastal freight is cheaper than road and still massively underpenetrated in India.

## Abhinav Parashar

Abhinav Parashar (https://www.linkedin.com/in/abhinavparashar/ - @{WhatsApp handle}) is the founder of Digio, the identity and documentation plumbing that sits under a lot of Indian financial onboarding. In 2009 he tried building an SMS-based P2P payment system for feature phones and learned that the software was the easy part; identity verification and regulation were what killed it. He shelved it, spent four years at Flipkart, and quit in 2015 after a Nandan Nilekani talk on India Stack convinced him Aadhaar had turned identity into an API call. He started Digio in 2016 doing Aadhaar eSign, back when a legally valid signature meant buying a DSC dongle. It now also runs eKYC (~40 checks, including video KYC) and eNACH recurring collections, and holds RBI payment aggregator and account aggregator licences. And the numbers are remarkable: ~₹150 crore ARR, ₹61 crore of profit, under 100 people, profitable nine years running and funded by its own cash, with Rainmatter (Zerodha) and Groww as both investors and customers.
