## The dedup status system

For the post's subject and angle, reconcile against the actual post archive first — `satish-hissa-linkedin-posts.md` and `satish-hissa-linkedin-posts-51-100.md` — via `project_knowledge_search`. Run **two searches minimum**: one on the company / subject, one on the angle. The archive is authoritative for what angle he has already taken.

Then assign:

- **OPEN ✅** — zero logged coverage of this company or this angle. Clean to draft.
- **CONTESTED ⚠️** — the company is new but the angle overlaps something recent, or the company appeared recently on a different angle. Draftable only with a genuinely different angle. Always name the overlapping prior post and its date in the tail so Akshay can judge the call.
- **COVERED ❌** — he posted this company or this exact angle recently, or the draft would merely restate a standing thesis. Do not silently draft a duplicate. Surface it and offer the closest OPEN angle instead.

A company name appearing in a past post does **not** automatically mean COVERED — check the angle that post took. Wonderchef can appear in a quick-commerce unit-economics post while a Wonderchef hiring-for-belief post stays OPEN.

## The standing-thesis trap

These are positions Satish returns to across his feed. A draft that only restates one of them is COVERED even if the company is new. Look past the company to the underlying claim:

1. Secondaries are quietly becoming the most consistent way employees see money, more than IPOs.
2. The old three-exit model (buyback, IPO, M&A) is breaking; secondaries are the emerging fourth pillar.
3. An IPO is the most visible but not the most common way employees realise ESOP value.
4. Partial sale (selling some, holding the rest) signals confidence; worry when insiders sell all.
5. Large ESOP expense / stock-based comp as a share of revenue is the clearest signal of real employee ownership.
6. Legacy finance can match RSU numbers on a spreadsheet but can't manufacture the feeling of building from zero.
7. Post-IPO, the question shifts from "when do we get liquidity" to "how do we time it" — sell a little, hold a little.
8. ESOPs are a wealth-creation tool, not just a retention tool; liquidity is what makes them real.
9. Quiet, capital-efficient liquidity (a small raise ending in a real cash exit) deserves more attention than loud headline offers.
10. Even when you understand ownership, you can still underestimate how much it compounds.
11. Ownership comes from taking risks that can fail, not from doing your job well.
12. Domain expertise can be a trap; hire for belief and cognitive flexibility, not pedigree.
13. Tribe-hiring (bringing the old team) buys speed but imports pre-built assumptions; strong cultures need diverse thinking to survive cycles.

If the only available angle for a new draft is one of these, it is COVERED. Tell Akshay which thesis it repeats, and either (a) offer a genuinely fresh angle on the same news, or (b) confirm whether he wants a deliberate reinforcement of a standing thesis (sometimes he does — but that's his call, not a default).

## What counts as a genuinely different angle

When the subject overlaps a past post (CONTESTED), a different angle means a different *load-bearing claim*, not a different example of the same claim. Test: would the two posts have different one-sentence insights (Step 1)? If yes, it's a fresh angle. If both reduce to "secondaries are how employees actually see money", it's the same post twice.

## Research / enrichment ladder

Enrich before drafting when the post turns on a quote, a number, or a recent event. Stop at the first rung that gives you enough.

**1. Project knowledge (transcripts and archive).**
- For **episode posts**: search the transcript by founder name + company + topic keyword for the exact quote and its surrounding context. Targeted range reads beat full re-reads. **Every quote goes in verbatim, verified against the transcript.** Never paraphrase a quote inside quotation marks. Never invent a quote, a number, or a detail the transcript doesn't support.
- For **dedup**: the post archive (covered above).

**2. Web search (news and figures).**
- For **news-reaction posts**: verify the load-bearing numbers, the date, and the named people against a primary source. Prefer the filing or company statement, then Business Standard, Entrackr, Inc42, Moneycontrol, ET, YourStory. 2-3 targeted searches is the ceiling.
- Confirm spellings of company and founder names. A wrong name or a stale number ships and gets caught publicly.

**3. Never fabricate.**
- If a number or quote can't be verified, leave a `{placeholder}` and flag it in the tail.
- **Public financial figures a founder cited** (revenue, ARR, valuation, raise size) get flagged for confirmation with the guest before publish — never passed through silently as fact.
- Distinguish a directly-sourced claim from an inference. If you're inferring, say so in the tail.

Do not disappear into research. If the data isn't surfacing after 2-3 searches, draft with placeholders and flag them. Akshay's time is more expensive than a clean round-trip.

## Sensitive-topic handling

Some on-beat stories involve a regulator, an investigation, a summons, a named individual's conduct, or unproven allegations (ED/FEMA actions, fraud probes, founder disputes, the recent ESOP-misuse ED reference). These are draftable but require care:

- Never imply guilt or wrongdoing about a named, listing-bound company or person.
- State only what is on record. If a founder was summoned and complied with nothing alleged, write exactly that and nothing beyond it.
- Flag the sensitivity in the tail with the safe framing spelled out, so Satish can see the boundary before he publishes.
- Satish's own handling is the model: on the ED ESOP-misuse reference he wrote "Regulators should go after the abuse. But genuine employee ownership should not become collateral damage." — supportive of clean policy, protective of legitimate companies, no accusation.
