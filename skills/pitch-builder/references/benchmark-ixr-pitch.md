**iXR Labs Podcast Plan**

*A B2B podcast, and the cross-platform content engine built around it.*

## Overview

iXR Labs builds AR/VR labs that give students real, hands-on practice without a real facility. In engineering and medicine, good labs are expensive, hard to scale, and always in short supply. That is the gap iXR closes.

iXR already has the product. The next step is building its presence with the people who decide what a campus buys: deans, directors, and medical heads. A podcast is one of the best ways to reach them.

## Why a podcast works for iXR

It does four things for you at once.

- A direct line to buyers. Invite a dean or medical director onto your show and a cold prospect becomes an hour-long conversation with you.
- You reach the whole room. The guest is the draw, but every other dean and department head listening becomes a warm lead who already knows iXR when your sales call happens.
- It builds real authority. Over a few months you become the show people associate with immersive education, the name they bring up first.
- One hour becomes a month of content. A single recording keeps every channel busy for weeks.

## Podcast concept

A narrow theme runs dry fast. Three interviews in and there is nothing new to say. So we build the show around the people, not a single subject. The range comes from the guests, and there is no shortage of them.

The show is a conversation with the deans, directors, and heads of department shaping how medicine and engineering are taught. Each episode is a different leader's world: the bets they are making, what is changing in their field, and how they think about preparing the next generation. It runs for years because every guest is different.

iXR stays in the room naturally, because immersive, hands-on learning is one of the threads the show keeps returning to, not the whole show. Across episodes the conversation can move through: how the best programs are designed and led, training students for the high-stakes moments that are hard to practise on, what AI is changing about the profession and about teaching it, giving every student access to top-tier facilities whatever the budget, and research moving into practice.

We judge the direction on two questions: will other professors and deans want to listen, and does it give iXR a natural reason to be in the conversation?

## The content engine

One recording turns into 18 to 22 pieces of content, enough to post three or four times a week across the iXR page and the host's profile.

Audio and video:
- Full episode for YouTube, Spotify and audio streaming apps
- 2 to 3 horizontal clips (3 to 5 min) for LinkedIn and X
- 3 to 5 vertical reels (30 to 90 sec) for Shorts and Reels

Written:
- Insightful posts derived from the podcast for LinkedIn and X
- Release posts ghostwritten for the iXR page and the host
- A LinkedIn newsletter and an X article from the discussion
- Threads from the conversation

Guest amplification kit:
- Ready-to-post copy for the guest's own LinkedIn
- A suggested post for the guest's university page
- Video and visual assets for every platform

Visual:
- Episode thumbnail
- 2 to 4 quote cards
- 1 carousel post
- Infographics

Sales enablement:
- Quotes and sound bites to send to the sales pipeline
- A newsletter for ongoing engagement with sales leads

## Timeline

Podcasts usually take about three months to start paying off. We ask for patience through the first quarter and consistency into the second.

- Phase one, setup (Month 1). Lock the concept, positioning, and look. Confirm the first guests. Build the recording setup.
- Phase two, first four episodes (Months 2 and 3). Start recording and publishing, and get the content engine running.
- Phase three, growth (Month 4 onwards). Once there is a library worth promoting, start paid distribution and let the audience build.

## Content builds the category

A podcast is one of the most reliable ways to build authority in a category. Show up with sharp conversations often enough, and you become the name people associate with the space.

It is a proven move in adjacent fields. Emversity, another company in the skilling world, uses podcast appearances to build its founder's profile, because the format earns attention and trust that ads and cold outreach struggle to match.

The opening for iXR is clear. No one in immersive education runs their own flagship show. The companies using podcasts today are appearing on other people's. iXR can own the format outright, and be first to do it.

## Total cost of running the podcast

- Months 4 to 6: monthly retainer plus a small performance marketing spend.
- Month 7 onwards: monthly retainer plus a small ad spend.

## How we measure success

- Host's LinkedIn visibility up 1.5 to 2x by the end of month six.
- YouTube channel past a real subscriber and view mark by month six.
- Company LinkedIn page engagement up 1.5 to 2x by the end of month six.
- Inbound requests from education-sector leaders who want to appear on the podcast by the end of year one.
