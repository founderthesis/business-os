# Standard pricing (current, October 2026)

Changed in the Drizz review, October 2026: guest sourcing ₹10,000 → ₹15,000 per guest
sourced; content ops ₹10,000 → ₹15,000 per month; offline recording redefined as
post-production only; a new third-party line for crew, equipment and studio rental;
studio setup consult rewritten to apply to remote hosts too and to say plainly that we
only introduce the consultant.

Pricing is dynamic. These are the current standard rates. In the Phase 3 pricing
gate, post this table in chat and ask the user to confirm or change it for the
specific client before building. Update this file whenever the rate card changes.

All figures in INR.

**A note on the word "cohort".** Everywhere else in this skill, cohort means the
buyers the show is aimed at. In this file and in the estimator it means a batch of
episodes, normally six or twelve. Where the two could be confused, write "episode
batch" in client-facing copy and keep "cohort" for the buyers.

**Offline recording is back on the card, in a different form.** A flat in-person
surcharge was removed in September 2026 because it fired on nearly every episode and
read as a hidden charge. It returned later that month as a per-episode line that is
Optional and **deselected by default**, which fixes the original problem: it only
costs anything on the episodes actually recorded in person, and the client switches it
on themselves. Do not reintroduce it as a blanket surcharge, and do not tick it by
default because a client mentioned wanting in-person episodes one day.

The trailer is a single line with no duration tiers. It replaced the earlier two
rows at ₹10,000 under 30 seconds and ₹15,000 for 30 seconds to a minute.

## Tag meaning

- **core (untagged):** the base you need to run the show. Setup and
  post-production. Neither is ever tagged Optional and neither is ever a toggle in
  the estimator: a pitch that lets the buyer switch off the thing that makes the
  show exist is a menu, not a pitch.
- **Recommended:** advised for a strong result, but the client can decline.
- **Optional:** available if wanted, easy to skip.

Guest sourcing is Optional by default, and some clients have it moved to core.

Tags are per client. Always confirm them in the Phase 3 pricing gate rather than
assuming the defaults.

## Rules for applying the card

**This file is the rate card.** If it cannot be read, say so and ask the user to
paste the current rates. Never reconstruct pricing from meeting notes, internal
call transcripts, or a previous client's note. Meeting notes are a draft of a
decision, not the decision, and reconstructing from them has produced wrong
figures before.

**Never invent a figure.** If a line cannot be grounded in this file or in
something the user confirmed, flag it in chat rather than shipping it. If a number
has to be in the document for it to make sense, say plainly which number was
picked rather than sourced.

**Drop lines that do not apply, rather than reframing them.** An adapted line at
the same price reads as padding. If the client has no fixed studio, the studio
consult comes out of the table. The reverse matters too: a client who is building an
in-house space wants that line and wants it explained, because most of them have
priced a studio build once and been quoted something frightening.

**The studio consult is a room, not a webcam.** It is the line clients misread most
often, because "studio setup" sounds like the kit list any producer would send. It is
for a company putting a permanent recording space into their own office: someone
specs the acoustics, the lighting, the cameras, the audio chain and the desk, sized
to the actual room, and hands over a buying list the client purchases directly. We
take no margin on the hardware. Say that in the line, because the alternative reading
is that ₹50,000 buys a microphone recommendation.

Where the client has no fixed space and no plan for one, the show records remotely
and this line does not exist. Where they are undecided, leave it in and tagged
Recommended, and say in chat that it is the one line that unlocks the in-person
episodes the rest of the roadmap assumes.

**The first three recordings free on "Akshay on the recording" is a concession,
not a default.** Offer it where a new host needs steadying, and confirm with
Akshay before it goes in a document. Concessions are always written as a count of
units, never as a period, because a count is what an estimator can subtract and
what a contract can enforce. Where it applies, the estimator charges only on
episodes beyond the free three.

**Watch for add-ons that will fire most episodes.** If a per-episode add-on
applies nearly every month, either fold it into the base rate or say so explicitly
under the table. A surcharge that applies to almost everything reads as a hidden
charge when the first invoice lands. This is why the flat in-person surcharge was
removed, and why offline recording came back as an optional line the client switches
on per episode rather than as a rate that applies to all of them.

**Offline recording stays off unless the client has said they want it.** It is the one
line most likely to be ticked out of optimism. A client who says they would like to do
some in-person episodes eventually gets the line in the table, unticked, and a
sentence in chat rather than a number in their estimate. If in-person recordings are
already agreed, tick it and set the estimator to the number of episodes actually
planned in person, never to the full batch.

**Guest sourcing is charged per guest sourced, never per episode.** Anyone the
client introduces carries no sourcing fee. Say this in the rate line itself, and
make any calculation model it: the sourcing quantity is episodes minus client
introductions, never the episode count.

**Every rate carries a period.** One-time, per month, per episode, per guest
sourced. A bare figure gets read the cheap way and argued about later. If a line
here is ever ambiguous, settle it with Akshay and fix this file rather than
guessing in a client document.

**Who does the arithmetic depends on the deliverable.** When the pitch ships with
the interactive page, the Word document carries the card and the page carries the
estimator, and no total goes into the prose: a fixed total under a modular table
invites an argument about assumptions the reader can settle for themselves. In a
Word-only pitch, give one total for the episode batch and state its assumptions:
setup, plus post-production and amplification across every episode, plus sourcing
on the episodes we source, and say what sits outside it, which is advertising
spend, optional lines, recording travel and taxes.

**When the card changes, sweep the build files.** Editing this file is half the
job. Grep `build_concept_note.js` and `build_pitch_page.html` for the old figure,
the old tag and the old concession wording too, because a rate card in a built
document does not update itself, and a stale concession line has shipped before.
