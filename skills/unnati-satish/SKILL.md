# Unnati posts promoting Satish / Built to Share — for a job-seeker audience

Turn a Built to Share episode into 2-3 LinkedIn posts that run on **Unnati's account** and promote **Satish Mugulavalli** and the **Built to Share (B2S)** page. The posts lead with the ideas from the episode, teach the reader something useful, and let Unnati's craft show through implicitly. Satish is the host who surfaced the thinking. The B2S page is where the reader goes next.

## The two things that make this skill different

**1. The audience is job seekers — not founders, not HR.**
This is the single most important constraint and the easiest to drift off. Built to Share is a show about ownership, ESOPs, talent, and org-building, and the obvious read of every episode is the founder's-eye or HR-eye view. That read is wrong for these posts. Reframe every idea to the candidate sitting across the table:
- Not "how to design a vesting schedule" → "how to read the vesting schedule in your offer"
- Not "how we structured the ESOP pool" → "what your ESOP grant is actually worth, and what to ask"
- Not "how we hired for a category that didn't exist" → "where your odds are best when your CV doesn't fit the mould"
The founder's lesson is the raw material. The job-seeker's takeaway is the post.

**2. Unnati promotion is implicit; Satish + B2S promotion is the point.**
These run on Unnati's account, but if they read as agency self-promotion ("look at the great content we produce for our client"), they die. The mechanism:
- Lead with the **idea**, taught well. The quality of the thinking *is* the Unnati ad.
- Name **Satish as host** and **Built to Share** as the show, naturally, as the source of the idea.
- End with the **B2S page/episode link** as the CTA so the reader goes to Satish's page.
- Never write a line about Unnati's services, production quality, or client roster inside the post.

## Input

- A Built to Share episode: a transcript, a newsletter draft, an episode-comments doc, or a prep/dossier note in the project.
- If Akshay names the guest/episode inline (e.g. "the Ankit / ideaForge episode"), use it. If not, search the project / Drive for the most recent B2S episode material and confirm before drafting.
- Akshay may paste a specific hook, a specific angle, or a quote he wants anchored.

## The workflow — gated

Two stops, then drafts. Do not skip the angle gate.

### Step 1 — Confirm the source and pull real quotes — HARD GATE

1. **Identify the episode.** If Akshay named it, confirm. If not, search the project files and Drive (`fullText contains 'Built to Share'`, guest name, `ESOP`, `ownership`) for the most recent episode's transcript / newsletter / comments doc, and ask which episode to anchor on. Do not draft on a guessed episode.

2. **Pull verbatim quotes from the source.** Read the transcript / newsletter / comments doc and extract the actual lines the guest or Satish said. **Every quote in a post must be verbatim from a source document.** If a vivid line you remember isn't in the docs you can see, do not use it as a quote — either find it in the transcript or write the point as paraphrase and flag it.

3. **Check struck-through / unconfirmed material.** Newsletter drafts often have struck-through passages or figures still being checked. Do not put struck or unverified numbers in a post. If a strong stat is unconfirmed, flag it in the tail note and offer to verify before it ships, rather than shipping it.

Then **STOP** if the episode was ambiguous. If Akshay clearly named the episode and the quotes are in hand, continue to Step 2 in the same turn.

### Step 2 — Angles (job-seeker framed) — HARD GATE

Propose the 2-3 post angles before drafting. Each angle must:
- Take one distinct idea from the episode (don't write three versions of the vesting post).
- State the **job-seeker takeaway** explicitly — what a candidate does differently after reading.
- Name the **ESOP/equity term** it will teach, if any.
- Name the **verbatim quote** it will anchor on (or flag "paraphrase, no clean quote").

Format:
```
**Post 1 — [short label]**
- Idea from the episode: [...]
- Job-seeker takeaway: [what the candidate does differently]
- Term taught: [vesting / cliff / ESOP / strike price / etc., or "none"]
- Anchor quote: ["verbatim line" — speaker] or [paraphrase, flag]

**Post 2 — [label]**
- [same fields]
```

Close with: *"Want all three, or swap any angle? I'll draft once you confirm."* Then **STOP**. Wait for Akshay.

### Step 3 — Draft the posts

Once angles are confirmed, **read `references/voice-and-format.md` before drafting.** It holds the hook patterns, the ESOP-explainer rule, the structure, the CTA shapes, and the banned-phrase / AI-tell library (Akshay's Voice DNA).

Write each post as a clean first draft. Output the post bodies only — no preamble inside them. Below the posts, separated by a line, add a short tail note that flags any unverified number (`{like this}`) and any place a quote was paraphrased rather than pulled verbatim.

Then stop and wait for Akshay's reaction. He edits from there.

## The non-negotiables (read before any draft)

Full detail and examples are in `references/voice-and-format.md`. The highest-leverage rules:

1. **Job seeker, not founder, not HR.** Every takeaway is something a candidate uses. If a draft is teaching someone how to *run* a comp plan, it's off-target — reframe to reading/negotiating one.
2. **Teach the term in plain words.** The first time a post uses ESOP, vesting, cliff, strike price, etc., it explains the term inside the prose, in one or two plain sentences. The reader is smart but may have never been granted equity. No jargon wall.
3. **Hook is a direct second-person question** in the Built to Share style. Reference pattern, from the Ravi / Wonderchef episode: *"Ever had a boss who measured you by process and not performance?"* Open most posts with an "Ever..." or equally direct "you"-question that names a situation the reader has lived.
4. **Quotes are verbatim, numbers are verified.** Never invent a quote. Never ship a struck-through or unconfirmed figure. Flag anything unverified in the tail.
5. **Promote Satish + B2S, keep Unnati implicit.** Name Satish as host and Built to Share as the show. End on the B2S page/episode link. Never write an Unnati sales line inside the post.
6. **Akshay's Voice DNA.** Short varied paragraphs. Contractions. Specifics over generalities. No em dashes. No banned AI vocabulary (delve, leverage, unlock, robust, seamless, and similar). No "It's not X, it's Y" negative-parallelism. No rule-of-three. No puffery. No engagement bait. Sentence-case headers if any.
7. **End with a "what this means for you" turn,** then the link. The reader should leave with one concrete action, not a tidy moral.

## Important behaviours

- **The gates are real.** Step 1 (confirm episode + pull quotes) and Step 2 (angles) are wait-for-Akshay gates when there's any ambiguity. Don't draft on a guessed episode or unconfirmed angles.
- **Default count is 2-3 posts.** If Akshay says a number, follow it.
- **If the source lacks a clean quote for an angle,** build the post from the documented fact and write the lesson as paraphrase, then flag it and offer to swap in the real line if Akshay pastes the transcript. Better an honest paraphrase than a fabricated quote.
- **B2S link format** is the built2share.in short link (e.g. `built2share.in/ankit`). Use the real slug if known; flag `{slug}` if not.
