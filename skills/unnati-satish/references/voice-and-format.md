# Voice and format — Unnati posts promoting Satish / B2S, for job seekers

Read this before drafting (Step 3). It holds the hook patterns, the ESOP-term explainer library, post structure, CTA shapes, the Voice DNA rules, and worked examples.

---

## The audience, restated

A job seeker reading LinkedIn. Could be early-career or mid-career. Smart, but may never have been granted equity and may not know what "vesting" or "ESOP" means. They are evaluating offers, or hunting, or wondering whether to stay. They are NOT founders and NOT HR. Every post earns its place by handing this person something they can use the next time they read an offer letter or apply for a job.

---

## Hook patterns

Open with a direct, second-person question that names a situation the reader has lived. This is the Built to Share house hook.

**Reference (Ravi / Wonderchef episode):**
> "Ever had a boss who measured you by process and not performance?"

Note what makes it work: it's specific (process vs performance), it's a real workplace experience, and it implicates the reader immediately. Match that.

Good shapes:
- "Ever accepted a job for the equity, then never checked how that equity actually reaches you?"
- "Ever turned down an offer over a small salary gap, without checking what the equity was worth?"
- "Ever been turned down because your CV didn't have the exact title they wanted?"

Avoid:
- Statement hooks that sound like a LinkedIn thought-leader throat-clear ("Equity is the most misunderstood part of compensation.").
- Engagement bait ("Agree? 👇", "Hot take:", "Unpopular opinion:").
- Rhetorical questions with no real situation behind them ("Ever wondered about wealth?").

After the hook, one short line that turns it toward the reader before the teaching starts.

## The ESOP-term explainer rule

The first time a post uses a piece of equity jargon, teach it in one or two plain sentences, inside the prose. The reader should never need a glossary. Keep it concrete and in plain words.

Mini-library (adapt, don't copy verbatim every time):

- **ESOP** — Employee Stock Ownership Plan. The pool of shares a company sets aside for employees. Being "granted options" means you're given the right to buy company shares later at a fixed, usually low, price.
- **Vesting** — the schedule by which granted shares actually become yours. A standard grant releases over four years, in equal slices. Until shares vest, you don't really hold them.
- **Cliff** — the catch at the start of a vesting schedule. A one-year cliff means if you leave inside the first 12 months, you get nothing. Stay past it and your first chunk lands at once.
- **Backloaded vesting** — a schedule where less vests in the early years and more vests later, so the reward tracks when value actually gets created. The opposite of a front-loaded grant that pays out most in the first year or two.
- **Strike price / exercise price** — the fixed price at which your options let you buy shares. If the company grows and the shares are worth more than your strike price, the gap is your gain.
- **Exercising** — actually buying the shares your options entitle you to, at the strike price.
- **Liquidity** — being able to turn shares into cash, usually at an IPO or a buyback or a secondary sale. Equity on paper isn't money until there's liquidity.

Rule: teach only the terms the post actually uses. Don't dump the glossary.

## Post structure

A reliable shape (vary it, don't make all three identical):

1. **Hook** — direct "you" question.
2. **Turn to the reader** — one line that says "here's why this matters to you."
3. **Teach the term** — plain-words explainer of the equity concept in play.
4. **The episode idea** — what came up on Built to Share, named to Satish + the guest, anchored on a verbatim quote.
5. **The reframe for a candidate** — what the founder-side insight means from the seat of someone reading an offer or job-hunting.
6. **"What this means for you"** — one concrete action: a question to ask, a thing to check, a place to aim.
7. **CTA line** — the B2S link.

Keep paragraphs short and varied in length. White space is your friend on LinkedIn.

## Voice DNA (Akshay's standing rules — enforce on every draft)

- Short, varied paragraphs. Contractions. Direct address. Specifics over generalities.
- **No em dashes.** Use full stops, commas, or restructure.
- **No banned AI vocabulary:** delve, leverage, unlock, robust, seamless, navigate (as metaphor), tapestry, realm, testament, and similar.
- **No negative parallelism / reframe constructions:** "It's not X, it's Y." Avoid entirely.
- **No rule-of-three** triads ("faster, cheaper, better").
- **No puffery** ("game-changing", "incredible", "powerful insights").
- **No engagement bait** ("Thoughts? 👇", "Agree?", "Repost if...").
- **Sentence case** for any header.
- Numbers serve the story; don't pile them on. One sharp figure beats five.

## Worked examples (from the Ankit Mehta / ideaForge episode)

These are calibration references for tone and structure, not templates to reuse wholesale.

### Example A — vesting, for job seekers

> Ever accepted a job for the equity, then never checked how that equity actually reaches you?
>
> Most offer letters say "4-year vesting" and most candidates nod along. Here's what that line actually means, and why it matters more than the number of shares.
>
> Vesting is the schedule by which your shares become really yours. A standard grant releases over 4 years, in equal slices. A cliff is the catch at the start: a 1-year cliff means if you leave inside the first 12 months, you walk away with nothing.
>
> That 4-year clock came from software companies that went public in 5 or 6 years. The schedule matched the exit.
>
> On Built to Share, Ankit Mehta of ideaForge explained why he didn't use it. ideaForge builds drones. They bootstrapped for 8 years before serious money arrived. The value showed up in years 5 and 6, not year 2. So he backloaded the vest.
>
> "It can be chunky but backloaded. Everyone starts creating value over a period of time, so it should be backloaded."
>
> What this means for you: if you're joining a deep-tech or hardware company, a front-loaded vest can quietly work against you, because you'll have left before your equity is worth much. Ask how the vest is structured, not just how many shares you're getting.
>
> Full conversation: built2share.in/ankit

Why it works: teaches two terms in plain words, anchors on a real quote, and lands a concrete action for the candidate. Satish + B2S named, no Unnati line.
