# Guest note: section spec, with the four worked examples

Four finalised notes. Where they agree, that is the house pattern. Where they
differ, the difference is explained, because the variation is usually the show's
situation rather than taste.

---

## 1. The hook

Two or three short paragraphs. A contrast or a premise, never a description.

| Show | Opening move |
|---|---|
| Human by Design | Contrast against the competition: most AI podcasts are hosted by people who never shipped a model. Then a two-word paragraph: "This one isn't." |
| Built to Share | Contrast between what founders usually talk about and what the best ones talk about |
| Playbooks by Prath | A premise that doubles as the format: playbooks aren't extracted in studios, so we come to you |
| Breakthrough Thesis | A premise about the world: breakthrough technology sits between working science and a sellable product, and the reasons are never scientific |

Write it last. You cannot write the hook until the rest of the note has told you
what the show is for.

## 2. What this is

The show named in bold, what it explores, who hosts it and who it is for. Two
paragraphs. Built to Share does this best: it names the decisions most founder
interviews skip, then says exactly which founders this is for.

## 3. What we talk about

Themes as bullets or as a numbered list, in the guest's language rather than the
producer's. Between four and six. Prath numbers them "1/ 2/ 3/", which reads
well and is worth borrowing.

Close the section on posture, not content. Two that work:

- "We focus on decisions, not outcomes. We make space for the real stories,
  including the uncomfortable ones."
- "We'd rather hear about the call you made when nothing was certain than the
  press release that followed."

## 4. Why come on

The section that does the actual persuading. Built from some combination of:

**The reach block.** Only when the numbers are real and confirmed.

| Show | Figure |
|---|---|
| Playbooks by Prath | 245k+ followers across LinkedIn, plus the audience profile |
| Human by Design | 240,000+ LinkedIn followers, plus the audience profile |
| Built to Share | 230k+ followers across LinkedIn |
| Breakthrough Thesis | None. New show, block omitted entirely |

Always pair the number with who those people are. A follower count on its own
means nothing to a guest deciding where to spend ninety minutes.

**Named early guests.** Built to Share lists four with a parenthetical on why
each matters (acquired by a listed company, unicorn, IPO bound, ~$50mn ARR).
This is the most effective substitute for reach that exists, and it is why the
fourth booking is easier than the first.

**What the episode is worth to them, made concrete.** Built to Share: the
episode becomes an employer branding asset for the careers page, hiring
conversations, social, and investor questions about culture. Prath: a walkthrough
of your space and prototypes is a moat that ages better than a press piece.

**Space to reflect.** Founders rarely get sixty minutes to think out loud with
someone who understands the game. Appears in three of the four.

**What gets published.** Prath lists it fully: long-form episode across
platforms, three to six vertical clips, insight posts and infographics, a
deep-dive newsletter tagged to the team.

## 5. Format and logistics

Bullets. What the guest actually needs to know:

| Item | Variation across the four |
|---|---|
| Where | Remote via Zencastr (HBD) or Riverside (B2S). On location at the guest's office, plant or lab (Prath). In person where the host travels, remote otherwise (BT) |
| Kit | Prath brings all equipment; guest provides space and time. BT specifies the setup and the guest's side runs the camera |
| Duration | Consistent: 90-minute block, edited to around 60 |
| Cadence | Bi-weekly (HBD), two a month (Prath, BT) |
| Preparation | Nothing to prepare, no script, no questions in advance |
| What they receive | Video, clips, transcript, thumbnails, a share kit |
| Control | Varies, and must be confirmed. See the Phase 2 gate in SKILL.md |

## 6. Meet the host

Photo beside text in a borderless two-column table. The bio has one job: prove
this host will understand the answer.

Three patterns:

- **Narrative** (Breakthrough Thesis, Built to Share). A paragraph or two that
  builds to why the questions land differently.
- **Credential blocks** (Human by Design). Research credentials, production
  experience, operating experience, each as a labelled bullet list. Best when the
  host's CV is the argument.
- **Fund-first** (Playbooks by Prath). The fund and its portfolio, then the
  career, then a one-line close: "Piyush has seen what works, what doesn't, and
  what we keep mistaking for one or the other."

Place it before Why come on when the host's name is the reason anyone says yes.
Place it after when the show's premise does more work than the host's CV.

## 7. Next steps

Human by Design numbers it: brief call to confirm fit, async preparation,
recording, release. Worth including where the process has more than one step
before the recording. The other three go straight to the closing line.

## 8. Closing line

One italic sentence. The actual ask.

- "If you've been through the trenches of building teams in India's startup
  ecosystem and have stories about how you won (or lost) the war for talent,
  let's talk."
- "If you are building or researching something at the edge of what is possible,
  get in touch and we will find a time."

Prath's runs longer and does a second job, naming both the guest's motive and the
ecosystem contribution. Either length works; the requirement is that it asks.

---

## What to omit and why

- **Hedged reach.** A new show says nothing about audience. "Growing" and "early
  but engaged" both read as zero.
- **Pricing.** Never. This goes to guests.
- **Promises the production team did not agree to.** Cross-promotion,
  turnaround, approval rights.
- **Anything the client asked to keep off the record**, including figures that
  came up in a recording.
