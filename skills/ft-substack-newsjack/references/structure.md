# The newsjack structure (proven — from the Epigamia piece)

This is the shape that works. It is not a news report with a podcast link bolted on;
it is **the news explained through the founder's own account**, with the archive as the
payoff. Follow the section order.

---

## 1. Title — the news, plus what only FT can add

Two moves in one line: state the news, and frame it with the insight the interview
gives you.

> **Rohan Mirchandani Built Epigamia to Outlast Him. A $20M Secondary Just Proved It Worked.**

Pattern: `{Founder} {did the durable thing}. {The news event} Just Proved It.`
Other shapes that work:
- `{Company} Just {news}. {Founder} Explained Why Three Years Ago.`
- `What {Founder} Told Us About {theme} — Before {news event}`

Keep the searchable entities (founder name + company) in the title. $10M rule applies.

## 2. Subtitle — dense facts, under ~155 chars

Names and numbers, no adjectives. It doubles as the search snippet.

> Epigamia's $20M secondary: Verlinvest and Rohan Mirchandani's family bought more as
> Deepika Padukone's fund sold, 20 months after his death.

## 3. Open on the news — dated, specific, and framed as a question

Don't warm up. State what happened, when, and the tension it creates.

> In December 2024, Rohan Mirchandani died suddenly at 42… Twenty months later, on
> August 25, 2026, his investors answered the question his death had left hanging over
> Epigamia. **They bought more of it.**

## 4. Unpack the news — the facts, cleanly

An H2 section that lays out who did what, with every name and number. Explain the
mechanics if the reader wouldn't know them (what a secondary is, what a down round
means). End on the question the facts raise.

> ## Who bought, and who sold

Close the section by turning toward the archive:

> The best answer on record comes from Rohan himself, in one of the last long
> interviews he gave.

## 5. Who the founder is — background, numbers, and internal links

Their history and the company's real figures. **Weave in 3+ links to related FT
articles** here — same category or same theme. This is where clustering happens.

> He belonged to a generation of founders rebuilding Indian consumer from first
> principles. [Shantanu Deshpande was building FMCG 2.0…](…),
> [Shankar Prasad was bootstrapping Plum…](…), and
> [Arman Sood was pioneering cold brew…](…).

## 6. The story from the interview — the verbatim quotes

The heart of the piece, and the part nobody else has. Use the founder's own words to
explain the thing now in the news.

> "We just thought we were kings of the world," Rohan told the Founder Thesis podcast
> about that early run. "We literally thought we were unstoppable, we'd become
> invincible."

Then draw out the operating principle the quote reveals — the method, the discipline,
the bet — and connect it back to the news.

## 7. Where the business stands now

Current, verified financials: revenue, losses, growth, distribution, funding to date,
who's running it. This is what makes the piece useful beyond the news cycle.

## 8. "The one source no other outlet has" — say it explicitly

Do not be coy about the wedge. Name it.

> Every report on this secondary is working from the same press release and the same
> cap table. Only one source explains why the family is buying: Rohan Mirchandani, in
> his own words, on how he built the company to last.

## 9. The CTAs

> **Watch the full conversation:** *{Episode title}*
> [Embed YouTube: {full youtube.com/watch?v=… URL}]
>
> **Read the full story of how he built it:** [{Article title}]({substack url})

## 10. Closing line — return to the opening frame

One or two sentences that answer the question the piece opened with.

> Most founders never find out whether their company can survive without them. On
> August 25, Rohan Mirchandani's did, and the people who knew it best chose to own
> more of it.

## 11. Sources

5–10 outbound authoritative links, named by publication.

> ### Sources
> - Inc42: [Verlinvest, Mirchandani Family Raise Stakes…](…)
> - Wikipedia: [Rohan Mirchandani](…)

## 12. The meta line

> *Tags: D2C, VC & Investing · Publish with no email · Embed YouTube + Spotify (<spotify episode url>)*

# Voice notes

- **Short declarative sentences.** The Epigamia piece lands its biggest beats in four
  words: *"They bought more of it."*
- **Never editorialise the numbers into a verdict.** Report "FY24 revenue edged up
  3.3% to ₹173.7 crore, net loss narrowed 74%" — don't add "which is disappointing".
- **Explain mechanics plainly** — a secondary, a down round, an ESOP buyback — without
  condescending.
- **Contrast is the engine.** Who sold vs who bought. What they said then vs what
  happened now. Build the piece on that tension.
- **Third person throughout**, no "I" or "we" except when quoting.
