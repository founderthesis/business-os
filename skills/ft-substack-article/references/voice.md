# The Founder Thesis house voice

The articles are written **in the first person, in Akshay Datt's voice**: he
interviewed the founder, he takes a stand, and the company's story is the evidence
for his thesis. They are not interview recaps. The storytelling craft is still
character-driven narrative nonfiction: you follow a real person through real
decisions, with tension, stakes and specific detail. Match the tone of Akshay's
LinkedIn newsletter "AI for the rest of us" (ask the team for the link if you
can't find it): direct, curious, opinionated, unimpressed by hype.

## What the voice does

- **Leads with a person or a tension**, not a company description. "At 28, an HR
  director's humiliation over his second-hand car at a posh Delhi school sparked
  the idea." Not "PeopleStrong is an HR-tech company founded in 2005."
- **First person, Akshay as the narrator.** "When I put it to him…", "I'm not
  fully convinced." The founder is named and the story stays about them; Akshay
  draws the conclusions. Use the guest's first name after the first full mention.
  No "in this episode" recaps.
- **Fact-dense but human.** Every paragraph carries a concrete detail — a number,
  a date, a named investor, a specific decision — but reads as a story, not a
  data sheet.
- **Present-tense hooks, past-tense narrative.** The opening can be vivid and
  immediate; the story is told in past tense.
- **Indian startup context** is assumed — readers know what a Series A, a D2C
  brand, or a unicorn is. Don't over-explain the ecosystem; do explain the
  specific business.

## What to avoid

- Summarising the episode chronologically ("First they talked about... then...").
- Marketing gloss ("revolutionary", "game-changing", "passionate") — show the
  thing instead.
- Long stretches of pronouns with no named entity (bad for answer engines and
  for the reader).
- Anything that reads as written for a search engine: question headings, keyword
  repetition, "(Read our piece on…)" asides.
- Em dashes in reader-visible prose, "it's not X, it's Y", and dramatic
  fragment triads.
- Padding to hit a word count. Depth means more real facts, not more words.
