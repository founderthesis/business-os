# Intent report — what ranks, what people search, how winners are written

Every claim here is measured from founderthesis.com's own Substack views, Google
Search Console, and the full 424-article corpus. Read this before writing so the
article is aimed at real demand, not a guess.

## 1. What ranks (the winning subjects)

The top-viewed articles cluster in three overlapping buckets — the same ones that
dominate Google search impressions:

- **D2C consumer brands** — Foxtale, Plum Goodness, Fittr, Epigamia, Arjun Vaidya
  / Dr. Vaidya's, Sleepy Owl. People search these by name constantly.
- **Controversy / rise-and-fall / famous names** — Ashneer Grover & BharatPe, the
  Reshamandi collapse, Kuku FM. High drama = high search.
- **Well-known founders & investors** — SquadStack, Scapia, Good Capital,
  Captain Fresh, Rebel Foods.

The median top-60 article earns **884 views**; the median bottom-60 earns **84** —
a ~10× gap. A dull B2B or services subject with a generic title is the typical
low performer. When you can lean into a recognisable name, a real number, or a
genuine turning point, do.

## 2. What people actually search (intent taxonomy)

From Search Console, traffic is almost entirely **founder / company name searches
plus a fact modifier.** Before writing, work out which of these intents apply to
your subject and make sure the article answers each one:

| Intent | Query shape | What the article must contain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | `[founder]`, `[founder] [company]`, `[company] owner`, `who is the owner of [company]` | Founder full name + role + company, stated up top |
| Wealth | `[founder] net worth` | Net worth / wealth if verifiable, or funding+valuation as proxy |
| Business | `[company] revenue`, `valuation`, `business model` | The real numbers and how it makes money |
| Background | `[founder] wikipedia`, `family`, `iit delhi`, education | Verified schooling, ex-employer, prior ventures |
| Status | `what happened to [company]` | For older/quiet/failed companies: the honest current status |
| Comparison | `[company] vs [rival]` | Where relevant, how it differs from the obvious competitor |

A **live** company draws revenue/owner/valuation queries; a **dormant or failed**
one draws "what happened to…". Match the article's framing to which it is.

## 3. The winning article shape (measured, top-60 vs bottom-60)

| Feature | Winners | Losers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Word count (median) | **1,594** | 336 |
| H2/H3 headings (median) | **6–7** | 0–1 |
| Has a subtitle | 65% | 25% |
| Colon in title | 58% | 28% |
| Number in title | 40% | 8% |
| Money figure in title | 32% | 3% |
| `Title \| Company` pipe format | 27% | **70%** |
| Block quotes (median) | **6** | 0 |

So a winner is: **~1,600 words, 6–7 plain editorial headings that name the entities, a `Name: arc + number`
title, a subtitle, and several verbatim quotes.** A loser is a ~330-word pipe-format
stub. This is the target every draft must hit.

## 4. The winning opening (from the top performers)

Two opening styles both work, and the best combine them — a hook that still lands
the founder + a headline number inside the first ~50 words:

- **Scene / moment led** — *"There's a moment in every founder's journey… For Apurv
  Agrawal, it came while standing at a window with a colleague who was smoking."*
  (SquadStack)
- **Bold-thesis led** — *"This is the story of a startup that embodied the
  venture-backed dream… It attracted over $70 million."* (Reshamandi)
- **Answer-first** — *"Meet Romita Mazumdar, the powerhouse founder and CEO behind
  Foxtale… investment banking at Bank of America Merrill Lynch."* (Foxtale)

Open with a hook, but never bury the identity and the number — the reader who
searched a name must see it answered immediately, and so must the search snippet.

## 5. The $10M rule for titles and thumbnails

A number only earns a place in the **title or thumbnail copy** if it is **≥ $10
million** (or the ₹ equivalent, ~₹80 crore+). Sub-$10M figures — a $1M seed, a
$4M bridge, a ₹5-crore round — are not impressive enough to headline and can make
FT look like it covers small deals. Use the biggest *legitimately impressive*
number: revenue, valuation, a large exit, users, GMV. If the only numbers are
small, lead the title/thumbnail on the story instead (the pivot, the comeback,
the famous name) — not on a small figure. Inside the body, all real numbers are
fine; this rule is only for the headline-level copy.
