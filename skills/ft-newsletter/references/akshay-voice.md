# Akshay's newsletter voice

Read before drafting. This is how Akshay sounds when he writes his own LinkedIn newsletter, "AI for the rest of us" (https://www.linkedin.com/newsletters/ai-for-the-rest-of-us-7398274614013063168), described by him as "Join me - a non techie - in my journey to master AI". Match this register in every Founder Thesis newsletter, whatever named voice is chosen.

## How he writes

- **Plain and conversational.** He writes the way he talks. "First - some context." "Now - coming to the main topic." Short paragraphs, everyday words.
- **Honest about himself.** He admits what he didn't know or got wrong: "I thought this would be the only tool I would ever need." "And let's be honest." This makes him relatable, not an expert talking down.
- **Everyday examples from his own life.** Finding a ramen restaurant near home that's open now. Packing for a trip to Tokyo. Looking for the next book to read. Big ideas are always shown through small, familiar moments.
- **A clear opinion.** He says what he thinks and why ("In my mind, Claude remains the best tool when the goal is writing"), then explains it simply.
- **Builds the story in steps.** He takes the reader through what happened in order: first this, then this changed, then he understood something new. The reader learns along with him.
- **Talks to the reader.** "Why do you use Google?" He asks the questions the reader is already thinking.
- **Invites a reply at the end.** "If you're still reading - then tell me if you agree with me - or do you use these tools differently?"

(Note: his own posts use dashes; Founder Thesis copy does not. Use commas or full stops instead.)

## Hooks from his Founder Thesis newsletters that worked

- "It is 10:15 AM on a Tuesday. You have ₹1 lakh in your trading app." (Meet the Heroes Behind India's Modern Equity Markets)
- "Somewhere this morning, a company paid two dollars to ask an AI how many letters are in the English alphabet." (Tokens are the new seats)
- "Somewhere in India tonight, a person is in real distress. They are not scrolling an app store." (India's Biggest Mental Health Company Is An Astrology App)
- "Somewhere right now, a procurement manager is opening a spreadsheet. She has paid the same invoice for three years." (The $30,000 Question Every SaaS Founder Is Dreading)
- "The broccoli in your pasta tonight started somewhere you will never see." (The Hidden Journey Behind Your Broccoli)

What they share: a specific moment, a person or "you", an everyday object, and a question the reader now wants answered. None starts with the company or the guest.
