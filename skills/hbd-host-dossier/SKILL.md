# hbd-host-dossier: Human By Design Host Dossier Pack

A pre-interview prep workflow for **Dr. Arjun Jain**, host of *Human By Design*. Arjun is an AI scientist (postdoc under Yann LeCun at NYU) and founder of FastCode AI. The show's editorial thesis is one line: **demystify hype from reality in enterprise AI**. The audience is CTOs, engineering leaders, AI practitioners and technical decision makers, roughly 255k on LinkedIn plus YouTube, Spotify and Apple.

## What this pack is for

**The job is narrow:** give Arjun a five minute skim that lets him run a high signal, technically grounded interview, and an instrument he can steer the recording with. Verified facts, flagged claims, and 6 to 8 theme blocks he can move between. It is NOT a research study, a biography, or a script.

## Eight operating principles

1. **Production truth over marketing.** Every theme points at the gap between what the guest says on a keynote stage and what happened in production. If a theme could be answered from the company's homepage, it is not a theme.

2. **Least provocative to most provocative.** The theme order is a ladder, not a pile. Theme 1 is a warm decode the guest enjoys answering. The last theme is the area they have **least addressed publicly**.

3. **Calibrate to the depth dial, not to the ideal guest.** Some guests are business impact people who defer mechanics to their team. Some are researchers who want the weeds. Set the dial in Phase 1 and write every question to it. Where a guest defers, do not write the question anyway and do not propose a follow-up episode. Episodes are one and done. Rewrite the question so the decision, not the mechanism, is the answer.

4. **Every theme needs a war story ask and an assumption challenge.** At least one question per theme asks what broke, what got killed, or what surprised them. At least one pushes against the accepted answer.

5. **Flag, never average.** Deployment counts, use case counts, headcount and "we skipped the pilot phase" claims are the recurring failure in secondary sources on enterprise AI. Where sources disagree, print both with a flag. Where a claim is self reported, mark it as material to pressure test on air.

6. **Tell the host what NOT to ask, not just what to ask.** For any guest who has given real long-form before, a dossier listing only questions is half a dossier. Naming the burned material costs under 200 words and is often the highest-value block in the file.

7. **Provenance travels with the claim.** See below. This is the rule that most often fails silently, and when it fails the host repeats a fabricated quote on tape.

8. **A contradiction is a theme, not a problem to solve.** When the same person has given two incompatible accounts, do not reconcile them in the dossier and do not pick one. Print both with sources and dates, and hand the reconciliation to the guest. Those are the best questions in the file.

## Provenance discipline (non-negotiable)

Every factual claim sits in one of three tiers, and the tier determines how it may be written.

**Tier A, primary.** Company releases, results statements, the guest's own published words, or reporting by a named journalist who was in the room quoting a named source. **May be stated flat.**

**Tier B, single or weak secondary.** One trade outlet, one profile, an SEO aggregator, an AI-written summary site, or a figure whose original source is not visible. **Must carry its source inline where it is used**, not only in a sources list. Never put a Tier B claim in the guest's mouth.

**Tier C, internal only.** Pre-call transcripts, guest manager emails, meeting notes. Unpublished, and usually the most valuable material in the pack. Three requirements: mark it unpublished, **supply the substance rather than citing the call**, and give the host a rule he can execute alone on tape.

Never make on-record use of Tier C material contingent on a clearance step. Chasing a comms team for permission is slow, signals that you have been briefing off an unrecorded call, and hands them a veto. The correct handling is always the same: **do not put the unpublished number or claim to the guest. Ask the question it belongs to and let the guest supply it.** If he does, it is his, on record, voluntarily. If he does not, the question still works and nothing has been lost.

### The aggregator trace rule

Aggregator and AI-written commentary sites routinely embellish. When a striking claim (a quote, a percentage, a named executive saying something vivid) appears **only** on such a site:

1. Find the primary it is built on, usually a wire story or a major outlet named in the piece.
2. Fetch that primary and check whether the claim is actually in it.
3. If it is: upgrade to Tier A and cite the primary.
4. If it is not: mark it **DO NOT USE ON TAPE** in the fact flags, and re-anchor any theme resting on it.

Mandatory for any claim load-bearing in a theme. A fabricated quote from the guest's own colleague, put to them live, ends the interview and the relationship.

### The cold-read rule

**Assume the host has read nothing except this pack.** Not the brief, not the pre-call notes, not the emails, and nothing about the company or the guest. He is a busy founder who opens this file thirty minutes before a recording.

That means, every time:

- **Never cite an internal call as though he attended it.** Name it plainly and date it ("On the prep call with [guest], [date]"), say it is unpublished, name who was on it, and give the substance in full. The producer writes this pack and hands it over, so first person plural is correct: "we are checking with his comms team", not "the producers are".
- **Never reference a claim by its label.** "The 20% stop rate is unpublished" is useless if he has never heard of the 20% stop rate. State the claim, then its status.
- **Every person gets an identifier on first mention.** Not "Cavendish" but "Will Cavendish, Arup's global digital services leader". This includes the production team: he may not know the producers' names.
- **Every company, product and venue gets a clause.** Not "the Arup fireside chat" but "a filmed fireside chat with the engineering consultancy Arup".
- **No internal machinery.** No version numbers of briefs, no "Tier B", no "repertoire flag", no references to the skill's own vocabulary.
- **Producer actions do not belong in the host's file.** "Confirm clearance with the comms team" is a producer task. Tell the host what to do if it is not cleared, and say the producers are handling the rest.

- **Do not announce the contract.** No "read this cold", no "this assumes you know nothing". Just write it that way. Telling the host he is ignorant is both patronising and a waste of the header.

Run this as a named check in the editorial pass: read the file top to bottom as someone who has never heard of the guest, and flag every noun that arrives without introduction.

## Close every loop

The pack ships complete. **No open items for anyone**, and in particular none for the producer.

- No "confirm X with the comms team", no "verify before use", no "still to check".
- Anything genuinely unverified is resolved inside the pack: either it is marked DO NOT USE and its theme re-anchored, or it is converted into a question the host asks on tape.
- Where two figures conflict, the resolution is "ask him for today's number", which is an interview move, not a task.
- If a fact could not be nailed down in Phase 2, say so plainly and give the host the workaround. Never leave it as an action.

A pack that generates follow-up work has moved the cost from the research to the producer, which is backwards.

## Do not hand the host homework

If the pack recommends the host bring something into the room, **draft it**. A framework to hand the guest, a taxonomy to react to, a counter-example, a prop. "Bring your own taxonomy" is an instruction; a five-tier taxonomy with worked examples from the guest's own portfolio is a deliverable. Same for behavioural advice: if the pack says "interrupt on a specific noun", list the nouns.

## Inputs

One or more of: guest name, LinkedIn URL, company or lab name, a PR backgrounder or a research export Akshay happens to have, a pre-call transcript or guest manager email (this outranks all secondary sources on scope and constraints), optionally a recording date, a flagged angle, or the depth dial.

Mine every upload FIRST. For `.docx`: copy to `/tmp/`, extract via `python-docx`, and read tables as well as paragraphs (briefs put their claim ledgers in tables). For mislabeled PDFs that are really ZIPs: copy to `/tmp/x.zip`, unzip, read the numbered `.txt` files. **Where PR framing conflicts with the pre-call, defer to the pre-call.**

If a prior version of a brief exists, treat the newest as controlling and say explicitly in Phase 1 which of your own earlier recommendations it overturns.

## Outputs

Generated in Phase 2 only, into `/mnt/user-data/outputs/`:

1. **Host Rundown (HTML)**: `<Lastname>_HBD_Rundown.html`. The reading and recording surface. Spec in `references/rundown-spec.md`. White background, dark text, one accent colour, never a dark theme.
2. **Host Dossier (Markdown)**: `<Lastname>_HBD_Dossier.md`. The canonical file for the producer and the archive. Spec in `references/dossier-structure.md`.
3. **Guest Prep Note (Markdown)**: `<Lastname>_HBD_Prep_Note.md`. Spec in `references/guest-prep-template.md`.

Then call `present_files` with the rundown first.

**Word bands, core dossier** (sections 1 through 6 plus flags): 1,200 to 1,500 standard, 1,900 to 2,300 when the repertoire flag is high. **Appendix blocks** (internal-call substance, the framework to bring, burned material, interview technique, time allocation, sources) sit outside the band, capped at 300 words each. `wc -w` counts markdown pipes and bullet dashes, typically 80 to 120 tokens of overhead; judge prose against the band, not the raw count.

## The workflow: one continuous run

Sequential but **not gated**. Claude does the research itself and ships the pack in the same turn. There is no prompt to emit, no external research tool in the loop, no pasting back, and no confirmation step. **Never stop mid-run to ask whether to proceed.**

The phases below are ordering, not handoff points. The only thing that pauses a run is a genuine ambiguity in the input, such as two people sharing the guest's name.

### Phase 1: Research

#### Phase 1a: Mine uploads, then run web_search

Mine backgrounders and pre-call material first. Then run 6 to 9 targeted searches:

1. `"<Guest>" <Company>`: current role, recent press
2. `"<Guest>" interview OR podcast OR keynote`: existing long form, what they repeat
3. `<Company> AI use cases production deployment scale`: operational receipts
4. `<Company> AI architecture OR platform OR stack`: build versus buy, named vendors
5. `<Company> engineering blog OR github OR open source`: culture and execution
6. `<Company> revenue OR headcount <current year>`: the numbers layer
7. `"<Guest>" failed OR killed OR "didn't work" OR lessons`: the war story hunt, highest value in the set
8. `<Company> partnership <named vendor>`: dependency and lock-in signals
9. For academics: `"<Guest>" google scholar` plus the lab page

`web_fetch` 2 or 3 substantive pieces: the most recent long-form interview, the most recent technical case study, and any coverage of a failure or a pivot. **Also fetch any vendor's own customer-story page about this company.** Vendor marketing is where executives say the quiet part about dependency, on the record, in a form they cannot disown.

#### Phase 1b: Set the technical depth dial

- **Dial 1, business impact.** Talks P&L, adoption, org design, payback. Mechanics deferred. Write about decisions and money. Take mechanics only as far as the decision behind them.
- **Dial 2, hybrid.** Comfortable at "we chose X over Y and here is why", not kernel internals.
- **Dial 3, deep technical.** Researcher or hands-on builder. Go to the mechanism, the benchmark, the failure mode, the paper.

Default to Dial 2. If a guest manager has flagged a constraint, quote it verbatim in the dossier header, since there is no separate research output for it to live in.

#### Phase 1b-ii: Set the repertoire flag

Independent of the dial. Count distinct outlets carrying a substantive interview, not mentions.

- **Low (0 to 3).** Binding constraint is information. Standard dossier.
- **High (4+, or bylined columns, or a company podcast they appear on).** Binding constraint is **redundancy and behaviour**. The failure mode is not a bad episode but a redundant one. The burned-material and interview-technique blocks become mandatory and the word band rises.

When the flag is high, Phase 1a gains a job: read at least one full existing long-form appearance and mine it for **behaviour** as well as content. What did the interviewer do that produced new material, and what produced the recital.

#### Phase 1c: Run the deep research sweep

Phase 1a maps the public set. This sweep goes after what is not on the map: vendor dependency, contradictions, the killed project, the question nobody has asked. Run it yourself from `references/deep-research-sweep.md`, which carries the ten targets, the query patterns and the standing rules. Budget 8 to 14 searches and 3 to 6 fetches on top of Phase 1a.

Two rules govern the sweep and both are load-bearing:

- **Where you find nothing, write "no sources found".** Never infer, never fill a gap with a plausible generality. An honest absence is usually a question for the host; an invented answer is how a fabricated claim reaches tape.
- **Run the aggregator trace rule the moment a striking quote or figure appears**, not later. Deferring it is how it gets skipped.

Where the sweep comes back thin, say so plainly in the fact flags and build the themes on what is solid. A thin-signal note is a legitimate output. A padded dossier is not.

Do not narrate the sweep to Akshay as it runs, and do not report search counts. Go straight on to Phase 2.

### Phase 2: Verify, draft, editorial pass, ship

#### Phase 2a: Gap-fill and the provenance sweep (3 to 6 searches)

Cross-check the sweep's findings against Phase 1a and close whatever the sweep left open. Then, before drafting:

- List every claim that will be **load-bearing in a theme**.
- Assign each a tier. Anything Tier B gets a trace attempt. Anything failing the aggregator trace rule is marked DO NOT USE and its theme re-anchored.
- Look for **contradictions in the guest's own account** across sources and dates. These become themes under principle 8.

#### Phase 2b: Draft

Follow `references/dossier-structure.md`. Read it before writing.

#### Phase 2c: Mandatory editorial pass (do NOT skip, do NOT show the pre-pass draft)

Re-read as an editor serving a host skimming on a phone 30 minutes before recording:

- **Skim test.** Absorbable in five minutes. Headings and bold carry the meaning.
- **Redundancy cut.** Each fact appears once, where it works hardest.
- **Ladder check.** Read the theme titles alone, top to bottom, and confirm the temperature rises.
- **Self-contradiction sweep.** Does any section assert something another section flags or denies? This is what provenance drift produces, and it hides across section boundaries.
- **Assertion audit.** Every flat statement in the analysis sections: is it Tier A? If not, it needs its source inline or a rewrite into a question.
- **Signal test per sentence.** If a sentence would be true of any enterprise AI guest, cut it.
- **Voice.** Colleague briefing a colleague.
- **AI tell strip.** No "delve", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "game changing", "deep dive", "unpack", "thought leader", "pioneering", "revolutionary". **No em-dashes** anywhere in any file.
- **Word count.** Check with `wc -w` against the bands.

Only the post-pass version ships.

#### Phase 2d: Rundown, prep note, checks, present

Build the HTML rundown from `references/rundown-spec.md`, generate the prep note from `references/guest-prep-template.md`, run the pre-delivery checklist, then `present_files`.

### Phase 3: Log the dossier in the Business OS (Notion)

The team works from Notion, so the dossier has to be findable on the guest's Engagement without anyone pasting links by hand. Run this after the dossier and guest note ship.

1. **Find the Engagement.** Engagements data source `collection://5522ee21-d8e5-4ec0-bf3e-90bcf88d24c5`, Human by Design rows only (`Show LIKE '%3ed19a301a7f815585e7e6c8accaab23%'`). Find the guest in People (`collection://e6c44319-7f81-4da6-8b80-8441a4875d7a`) by LinkedIn first, then exact name, then match `Guest LIKE '%<people page id>%'`. If there is no match, or more than one open Engagement could be it, do not guess and do not create one: say so in the chat reply.
2. **The link.** Use the shareable link to the dossier: the published page URL if it was published, otherwise this chat's URL if you have it. If there is no link you can give, leave Dossier link alone and say "add the dossier link by hand" in the reply.
3. **Write.** `Dossier link` = the link. Add one line at the top of `Claude note`: `YYYY-MM-DD: Arjun's dossier and guest prep note ready (<link>)`, keeping existing lines below it. `Claude updated` = today. Do not change Status, Next action, Tier, Payment or anything else: the team sends the guest note and moves the status.
4. **Reply.** End the chat reply with one line: `Notion: Dossier link added to <Engagement name>` (or what failed). A failed Notion write never blocks shipping the dossier.

The calendar routine copies the Dossier link into Akshay's private prep entry on its next run.

## Dossier section structure (full spec in `references/dossier-structure.md`)

1. **Header**: guest, role, recording target, format line including the depth dial.
2. **The One-Line Hook**: ≤45 words, at least one hard number.
3. **§ 1 Guest Snapshot**: 130 to 170 words, reverse chronology, the earned secret, public footprint.
4. **§ 2 Company or Initiative Overview**: 180 to 220 words in bullets.
5. **§ 3 Key Metrics**: compact table. Year and source type on every number.
6. **§ 4 The architecture question**: 100 to 250 words. One or two non-obvious decisions: what they chose, what it cost, what they gave up. Where accounts conflict, this section becomes the contradiction, printed with both sources.
7. **§ 5 Culture and Execution Signals**: 60 to 80 words.
8. **Contested numbers**: NOT a standalone warnings section. A host who has never heard of a claim cannot be warned off it, and listing it only introduces him to the risk. Handle it two ways instead. Soft or conflicting figures get a short plain-English note directly under the metrics table saying which are soft and why, and which are unusually solid. Anything that is genuinely a good question gets converted into a question inside the relevant theme, with the context built into the question itself ("an MIT Sloan write-up put you at nearly 100 use cases; that came from the authors, not from you; what counts as a use case?"). Anything failing the aggregator trace rule is **deleted from the pack entirely**, along with any theme resting on it. Do not warn the host about a claim you have removed.
9. **Internal-call substance** *(when Tier C material exists)*: the unpublished material in full, marked unpublished, with clearance status.
10. **⛔ Burned material** *(high repertoire only)*: 8 to 14 one-liners, how each is burned. BEFORE the themes.
11. **How to interview this one** *(high repertoire only)*: Works / Fails / The big one / Do not go here, plus an editor note. BEFORE the themes.
12. **The framework to bring** *(when the guest responds to being handed one)*: drafted, not delegated.
13. **Time allocation**: table mapping minutes to themes when the recording has a hard stop.
14. **§ 6 Themes**: 6 to 8 blocks, ≤100 words each, on the ladder. Add **Closing beats** after the final theme for strong questions with no natural rung.
15. **Sources**: clickable, verified, triaged into *watch before the record*, *the ones that power the closing rungs*, and *reference*.

## The theme ladder (the core IP)

1. **The decode.** What the thing actually is. The easy on-ramp.
2. **The build.** The decision that was not obvious, and what it cost.
3. **The scale reality.** What changed in production: latency, cost, data quality, the org.
4. **What died.** The killed use case, the failed pilot. The war story rung.
5. **The business layer.** Who pays, what payback looks like, how a project gets killed.
6. **The uncomfortable one.** The dependency, the lock-in, the question they have dodged. **Always last.**

For professor-founder guests, rung 6 is usually: is the company a thin wrapper on the lab, does the research transfer, or who owns the IP. For corporate guests it is usually the vendor dependency underneath a product they brand as their own.

Add rungs 7 and 8 only when the guest has genuinely more surface. Never exceed 8. Never let a provocation land before the on-ramp.

## Defensive notes: what NOT to do

- **Don't skip Phase 1c.** Phase 1a alone produces a competent summary of the public repertoire, which is the one thing this pack is not for. The sweep is where the vendor dependency, the contradiction and the killed project come from.
- **Don't stop to hand research out.** No Perplexity prompt, no Gemini prompt, no "paste the results back". Claude runs the searches and ships in the same turn. If Akshay pastes his own research in, treat it as an input to verify, not as a substitute for the sweep.
- **Don't pad a thin sweep.** Say the signal is thin, build on what is solid, and let the gaps become questions.
- **Don't state a prep-call claim flat.** It is one unrecorded conversation, often auto-summarised, and auto-summaries garble names and numbers. Mark it unpublished and say so.
- **Don't trust a striking quote that lives on one aggregator.** Trace it. If the primary does not contain it, it probably does not exist.
- **Don't reconcile the guest's contradictions for them.** That is the interview.
- **Don't open on the provocation.** It inverts the ladder and burns goodwill in minute three.
- **Don't write questions the guest will duck.** Rewrite them so the decision is the answer. Never propose a follow-up episode as the fix: every booking is one and done.
- **Don't take deployment counts at face value.** "100 use cases in production" is exactly what this show exists to test.
- **Don't write a section that warns the host off facts he does not have.** If he needs to know a number is soft, say so where the number appears. If a claim is unusable, remove it rather than flagging it.
- **Don't let themes go generic.** "AI governance" is a category. "Why the procurement use case died on supplier data structured by legal entity" is a theme.
- **Don't include awards, citations, or rankings.** Arjun is not moved by them and they cost 40 words.
- **Don't put behavioural guidance inside a theme.** Pace and question length apply to the whole recording and belong in their own block.
- **Don't put specific questions in the prep note.** Territory only.
- **Don't promise content approval or edit rights in the prep note** unless Akshay says so for that guest.
- **Don't do BD scoring here.** That belongs in `hbd-guest-eval`.
