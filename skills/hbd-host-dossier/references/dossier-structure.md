# Dossier structure spec (Phase 2b)

Markdown. Sections in this order. Bracketed sections are conditional.

---

## Header
Guest, role, employer. Recording date, duration, location. Format line carrying **both** the depth dial and, if high, the repertoire flag. One sentence on what the dial means operationally, e.g. "Every question needs a decision at the end of it."

## The One-Line Hook
≤45 words. The single most defensible claim this guest owns, with a number attached. Not a summary. The thing that makes the booking interesting.

## § 1 Guest Snapshot: 130 to 170 words
Reverse chronological. What they shipped, not what they studied. Then a bolded **earned secret**: the one non-obvious thing about how they got the job or how they think, which sets the register for the whole interview. Then **public footprint** in one line: which outlets, and whether the repertoire is heavy.

No awards. No citations. No rankings.

## § 2 Company or Initiative Overview: 180 to 220 words, bullets only
What they build and sell, the differentiation, the traction, the last 12 months. Each bullet leads with a bolded noun phrase. Include the commercial weather (results, guidance, funding) as a bullet, even for a guest who will not discuss money, because the room knows.

## § 3 Key Metrics: table
Every number carries a year and a source type. Use ⚑ inline on any figure that is contested or self-reported. Where a figure has multiple circulating values, print the range and mark it rather than picking one.

## § 4 The architecture question: 100 to 250 words
One or two non-obvious decisions: what they chose, what it bought, what it cost. Not a technology list.

**If the accounts conflict, this section becomes the contradiction.** Print each version with its source and date, say plainly that they do not agree, offer the likely reconciliation as a hypothesis only, and instruct the host not to narrate either version at the guest. Ask the guest to draw the line.

## § 5 Culture and Execution Signals: 60 to 80 words
Engineering blog, open source, hiring language, team structure, named partners. A null result is a finding: "no open source, no engineering blog, no papers as a recruiting surface" tells the host what kind of organisation this is.

## ⚑ Fact Flags: 3 to 7 bullets
Each actionable on air. Include any **DO NOT USE ON TAPE** item in bold at the top of the list, with the reason and what replaced it. Flags that only say "this is uncertain" are wasted; each should tell the host what to do about it.

## [Internal-call substance]
When Tier C material exists. Open with one line stating the host was not on the call and that none of it is public. Then the substance in bullets. Note clearance status on anything sensitive.

## [⛔ Burned material]: high repertoire only
8 to 14 one-liners. Each: the item, then in smaller type how it is burned (how many outlets, which venue). Where a public version exists but a sharper private version does not, say so and point at the theme that uses it. **Before the themes.**

## [How to interview this one]: high repertoire only
Four parts. **Works:** moves that produced new material in their existing long-form. **Fails:** question shapes that produced deflection or recital. **The big one:** the single behavioural instruction that matters most, usually pace, interruption or question length. **Do not go here:** stated vows, canned answers, standing refusals. Close with a pronunciation or audio note for the editor. **Before the themes.**

## [The framework to bring]
When the guest is the type who engages with a handed framework. Draft it: three to six tiers or categories, each with a worked example drawn from the guest's own portfolio, then a bolded "the move" line explaining what it cuts across in their public framing.

## [Time allocation]
Table: segment, minutes, theme numbers. Only when the recording has a hard stop.

## § 6 Themes: 6 to 8 blocks, ≤100 words each
Each block: title, one or two sentences of why this matters and what is unaddressed, then 2 to 5 questions as a bulleted list. Attach ⚑ notes to individual questions where a clearance or provenance caveat applies at the moment of asking, not in a separate section.

Ladder order is non-negotiable. Read the titles alone top to bottom and confirm temperature rises.

**Closing beats:** after the final theme, 1 to 3 strong questions that have no natural rung.

## Sources
Clickable, verified, triaged into three groups:
- **Watch and listen before the record**: video and audio the host should consume, with links
- **The ones that power the closing rungs**: the two or three sources the hardest questions rest on, with the key wording summarised so the host can put it accurately
- **Reference**: everything else

Write "(verify live)" rather than faking a URL.
