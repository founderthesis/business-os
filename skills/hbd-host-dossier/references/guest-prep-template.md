# Guest prep note spec (Phase 2d)

Markdown, 480 to 560 words, addressed to the guest. Warm, peer to peer, substantive. Never nervous-making.

## Structure

**Title line.** Show name, then "Preparation Note", then guest name and recording date.

**The show: ~60 words.** What it is, who listens, and one line establishing that the audience is practitioners rather than a general audience.

**Your host: ~90 words.** Arjun's background, told for what it means practically: he will not need concepts explained. End with the payoff sentence, e.g. "you will not have to explain why the last ten percent takes a year."

**[What we have already read]: ~60 words.** Include when the repertoire flag is high. State that you have gone through their existing long-form and are deliberately not going to repeat it. This is worth real goodwill with a comms team and it is also true.

**The territory: ~180 words.** Four or five areas in broad strokes. Bolded lead phrase, then one sentence. **No specific questions.** A guest in prep mode kills the thing that makes the episode good.

**Format: ~90 words.** Duration, unscripted, no question list in advance, that the host will interrupt to chase detail and that this is a compliment. Edit length. What they get afterwards (clips, thumbnails, short videos). Recording setup guidance: Riverside, Chrome, wired headphones, stable connection, short check before rolling. Adapt if the guest's own team is providing a studio.

**Close: ~40 words.** Nothing to prepare. Then the one concession that is always safe: if there is a subject they would rather avoid, tell us before we roll and we will keep away from it.

## Hard rules
- **No specific questions.** Territory only.
- **No content approval, edit rights, or transcript review** unless Akshay says so for that guest. If he does, phrase it as a courtesy, not an entitlement.
- **No em-dashes.**
- Do not describe the themes in the sharp language the dossier uses. "Governance and honest limits" not "the number you put on stopping."
- Do not flatter. Do not oversell the audience size beyond what is true.
