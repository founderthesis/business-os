# Founder Thesis — episode positioning themes

Themes are the reasons a stranger would give up an hour. They are not summaries. A summary says what the episode contains; a theme says why that matters to someone who has never heard of this founder.

This runs first in the working doc. Everything downstream selects from what you produce here, so breadth matters more than polish.

## Inputs

- **Transcript** (required). Read it fully. Themes drawn from a summary are always generic.
- **Research dossier** (optional but strongly preferred) — market size, competitors, funding, recent news.

## What to produce

**8 to 10 themes.** For each:

- **Theme title** — the strategic angle, stated as a positioning line, not a topic label
- **Summary** — what this theme highlights, in two or three sentences
- **Relevance** — tie it to a megatrend, a disruption, a policy change, or a market cycle. If you cannot name the external force that makes this interesting *now*, the theme is weak and you should say so
- **Alignment** — how closely it tracks what was actually said. Rate Excellent / Very good / Good / Moderate, and name the caveat where one exists
- **Best format** — LinkedIn post (200-300 words) or newsletter (1,000+ words)

Keep every theme easy to understand. If a theme needs a paragraph of setup before it lands, it will not survive a scroll.

## Spread the themes deliberately

Do not produce eight variations of one idea. Cover:

- **The scale angle** — the market, the number, the national frame
- **The contrarian angle** — where this founder disagrees with consensus
- **The operating angle** — the mechanism, the unglamorous decision that actually worked
- **The founder angle** — the failure, the pivot, the years in the wilderness
- **The narrow-but-deep angle** — the one that only 5,000 people care about and all five thousand will watch to the end

The narrow one matters. It is usually the theme that produces the best-retained episode, and it is the one a generalist brief will talk you out of.

## What the channel data says about pull

Founder Thesis performance evidence, current as of the FT Channel Rules page (https://app.notion.com/p/3d219a301a7f813fa919fe4b6c48a72f — read it, it supersedes this section):

- **Consumer and D2C framings win the click** (+20% click-through against what reach predicts)
- **Fintech framings hold the audience** (+19% retention) even though they do not win the click
- **Investor and VC framings underperform on both** — if the guest is an investor, find the operating story underneath rather than leading with the fund

Flag any theme that leans on a "what VCs look for" frame. It is the most reliably weak angle in the catalogue, and it is the one that comes most easily.

## Hard rules

- **Never invent a fact to make a theme work.** Every claim traces to the transcript or the dossier.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas or simple dashes.
- **Name the weak ones.** If a theme has broad appeal but thin transcript support, say so plainly and rate the alignment honestly. A theme that oversells produces a title that oversells, which produces a viewer who leaves at ninety seconds.
- Do not write finished copy here. Themes are raw material.
