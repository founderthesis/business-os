# Piyush Goenka — Global D2C Case Study Post

Convert a single global D2C brand's story into a finished LinkedIn post in Piyush Goenka's voice (co-founder, Prath Ventures). The post is a **case study of the global brand**, ending with **one short lesson for India**. The benchmark is Seth Godin clarity — it must read like a sharp investor who studied this brand wrote it, not like content was produced.

This skill is **standalone**. It does not depend on the `piyush-linkedin-post` skill. Everything needed to draft is here.

## What this skill is for (and what it isn't)

**Use this skill when** the post is built around **one global brand** — its founder, product, the consumer insight it was built on, how it scaled, how it exited — used to draw a transferable lesson for an Indian founder. Examples: Siete Foods, Kodiak Cakes, Olipop, Liquid Death, Athletic Greens, Graza, Magic Spoon, Yeti, Chamberlain Coffee.

**Do NOT use this skill for:**
- Indian-category theses (multiple Indian players, Indian white spaces) — that's a different format, India-weighted.
- Weekend musings, decision-making essays, deal announcements, or operational posts.
- Posts that merely mention a global brand in passing.

## The two governing instructions (Piyush, June 2026)

These are why this skill exists. Both came directly from Piyush rejecting earlier case-study drafts.

### 1. The brand is the subject; India is the side note.

> "we need to really up the research that is done on each of these companies.. too much has been written about indian market rather than about [the brand] and its reasons for success. indian market has to be side note as learning from the global success."

If the body is more about India than about the brand, **the post is wrong.** The reader should finish knowing something specific they didn't about *the brand* — its founder's origin moment, the product decision that mattered, the consumer insight underneath it, how it scaled, what the acquirer was really buying. India is the closing lesson, not the body. Target weighting: the brand's story is ~70% of the post; India is ~30% or less.

### 2. Recency — soft flag, not a wall.

> "can we first focus on more recent success.. brands started in the last 5 or 10 years.. as trends change really fast and hence talking about something which got acquired 5 years back seems really old story."

Apply as a **soft flag**:
- **Preferred:** brand scaled or exited in the last ~5 years (≈2021 onward), or founded in the last ~10 years.
- **Borderline / older (5–10 years back):** flag in the tail before drafting and offer a fresher comparator, then **draft if Akshay confirms.** Never silently draft an old story; never silently refuse one.
- **Clearly old (10+ years, or a story told to death):** name the concern, offer 1–2 fresher same-category comparators, still draft if Akshay insists.

The gate is a conversation. Akshay decides.

## Input

Anything from a one-line topic ("case study on Olipop for Piyush") to a pasted backgrounder on the brand to a Gemini Deep Research report. The user may also paste:
- A specific India angle the lesson should land on
- Data to embed verbatim (founder names, exit figures, portfolio context)
- Tone overrides ("no hashtags", "no team tag", "shorter than usual")

## The non-negotiables (Voice DNA)

If a draft fails any of these, it does not sound like Piyush.

1. **First sentence does work.** No throat-clearing. The opener is a thesis claim, an aphorism, a paradox, a number, or a dated scene that contains the brand — never "In today's fast-paced world" or "I want to share something about". 64% of his openings are declarative.

2. **Short paragraphs are normal.** Median paragraph ~20 words. Single-sentence paragraphs function as pivots and aftershocks. Every post over 200 words should have at least 2 single-line beats.

3. **Concrete over impressionistic.** Real dated numbers ($15M → $200M, $1.2B acquisition, 2025). Named founders (full names, correct spellings). Named cities. Vague words like "many", "thousands of", "the youth" read as not-Piyush. Name the number; name the person.

4. **The Indianness rule.** At least one Indian proper noun (brand, founder, place) in the opening ~100 words, woven in as support — the global brand stays the subject. The India lesson lands at the close, naming 2–3 real Indian founders/brands. Never an all-Western post with zero Indian anchoring.

5. **Tricolon rhythm.** Three-beat sentence patterns. *"Clean-label. Unmistakably Mexican-American. Neither diluted."* At least one tricolon per post.

6. **Closes on a statement, not a question.** 73% of his closes are declarative. Default to an aphoristic compression that echoes/sharpens the opener. Don't reach for "What do you think?".

7. **Calm register.** No exclamation marks. No hustle voice. No motivational bullet stacks. No "Founders, here's the truth no one tells you". Measured, observational, elder-statesman.

8. **Hedges live in the ledger, not the prose (July 2026).** Verify hard in the fact-check gate, then write declaratively. A post stacked with "as she has said", "roughly", "reportedly" reads cautious and flattened his approval of the Bubble draft's confident register confirms he does not want it. One precise hedge on a genuinely soft figure ("an estimated") is fine; a hedged register is not. The rigour ships in the research-provenance block, invisibly. This raises the stakes on the gate itself: a declarative sentence resting on an unverified figure is his name on a public error, so nothing skips verification — the verification just leaves no fingerprints on the copy.

9. **The personal-stake close (July 2026).** The strongest India turns land on something Piyush has personally seen — a family observation, a founder meeting, a shelf he stood in front of. The Bubble draft's teenage-daughter line is the exemplar: it turned the India section from analysis into conviction. **Hard guard: personal details are drawn ONLY from the verified personal-details bank (pending from Piyush) or from something he has said in a transcript, meeting note, or WhatsApp message in the project files. Never invent a personal fact.** Until the bank exists, a proposed personal-stake line goes in the flagged-items tail as "PERSONAL CLAIM — confirm true before publishing", stated as such, every time. A fabricated personal detail that ships is the single fastest way to destroy the trust this whole system runs on.

## The theme palette (a checklist, not a fixed order)

Piyush's exact list of themes a case study should "capture most of":

- Founder story
- What the business is
- Product story
- Core consumer insight it was based on
- Positioning
- Core consumer proposition
- Reason to win
- Scaling engine
- Exit / end scale / valuation
- Lesson for India market — consumer behaviour change, how to win in India

**Required spine element (July 2026): the character-revealing moment.** Every case study needs one moment where the brand paid a visible cost to act on its values or its consumer insight — the beat that turns analysis into a story. Exemplar: Bubble telling eight-year-olds on TikTok not to buy its exfoliating products during the Sephora Kids wave, warning off its own fastest-growing segment. The research phase hunts for this moment explicitly (founder interviews, controversy coverage, decisions that cost revenue). **The spine does not lock without one.** If research surfaces no such moment, flag it in the tail before drafting rather than shipping a post that is all mechanism and no character.

His caveat: *"each brand might need us to focus on one or the other part more.. it doesnt have to follow the exact pattern each time."*

So **draw from these; don't march through them.** A post should capture *most* of them, lean harder on the 1–2 that carry this brand's story, and compress or skip the rest. A draft that mechanically goes founder → business → product → insight → positioning → proposition → reason-to-win → scaling → exit → India in fixed order reads as a template and fails the edges-over-polish bar. Pick the spine, then arrange around it.

## Workflow

### Step 0 — Dedup gate (run before anything else)

Before drafting, pull the live record from Google Drive: use the Google Drive connector to search `title contains 'Piyush — Recently Published'` (in the "Piyush Content Ops" folder) and open the **most recent** dated one. Classify the proposed post by **Sub-category** and **Success factor** using the tag vocabulary in that file. Compare against the last ~8 weeks:

- Same Sub-category within ~6–8 weeks → 🚩 RED FLAG.
- Same Success factor within ~3–4 weeks → 🚩 RED FLAG.
- Both match → 🚩 HARD STOP (near-duplicate).

If flagged, stop and tell the writer which published post it clashes with (title + date) and ask them to space it out or reposition. Do not proceed past a flag without an explicit override. Full procedure: `DEDUP_CHECK.md`.

Also enforce the fact-check gate: every factual claim verified against a primary source before the draft is final (see Step 3's grading gate and the `fact-check` skill).

### Step 1 — Find the insight first

Read the input and find the **one sentence the post is really about** — the load-bearing claim about *why this brand won* that doubles as a lesson. This becomes the opener or the close, often both. Examples:

- Siete → *Heritage is not the obstacle to health. It is the moat.*
- Kodiak → *Reframing the same product as protein-first moved it from a price fight to a premium shelf — and made it a platform.*

If you can't find a single insight, ask the user before drafting. Two-insight drafts read as muddled.

### Step 2 — Run the recency check

Identify the brand's defining moment (scale inflection or exit) and its year. Apply the soft flag from "Governing instruction 2" above. If borderline/old, flag and get Akshay's confirmation **before** emitting the research prompt or drafting.

### Step 3 — Research gate (brand-first)

The single biggest delta between a weak case study and a Piyush-grade one is **depth on the brand**. If the brief is missing the brand's founder story, product decision, consumer insight, scaling engine, or exit detail, fetch them before drafting.

#### The depth doctrine (Piyush, June 2026)

> "I'm not seeing an improvement in the quality of research. I read it and I feel it's cursory and that's not my style."

> "it's not just AI. it's kind of reading up of even 15-20 minutes of generative reading on them before we go to AI and ask them to make a post.. there would have been hundreds of hours of research in a Wall Street Journal podcast before it went live. we are not seeing that research."

Two failures, two fixes:
- **The research is shallow** — Wikipedia-depth summary, no mechanism, no primary sources. Fixed by a harder prompt and a grading gate (below).
- **Even when research is done, Piyush can't see it, so he re-verifies it himself.** Fixed by the **research-provenance block** that ships with the draft (Step 7).

The benchmark is explicit: the work should read like a WSJ research desk stood behind it. The standard is **15-20 minutes of generative reading on the brand before AI formats anything.** And the differentiator is **mechanism** — the scaling engine explained step by step (*how* they scaled), not as outcomes (*that* they scaled). "DTC then retail" is not a mechanism. "Seeded N micro-creators, converted to retail via X, the wedge was Y" is. This is the section Piyush misses most.

**Operational density quota (July 2026): minimum three machine-room specifics per case study** — details from the layer below press coverage. Team composition (two chemists, three product developers, eight in-house dermatologists), iteration counts (a hundred formulations on one serum), process mechanics (a 90% community approval threshold with a real veto). These are what make a post read reported rather than summarised, and they are what Piyush approved wholesale on the Bubble draft. The deep-research prompt must ask for them by name; a brief with none of them fails the depth gate even if the revenue arc and exit figures are solid. **De-prioritise deal-desk detail** (banker names, process mechanics of a sale, "strategic options" coverage) — it earned nothing with him and crowds out the machine room.

**Escalation ladder — stop at the first that gives you enough:**

1. **The brief itself.** If Akshay pasted a rich brand backgrounder covering most of the brand-themes, the research is there. Run it through the scorecard below; ask only for specific gaps.
2. **Web search.** 2–3 targeted searches for the brand's founder, revenue trajectory, funding, and exit terms.
3. **Deep-research gate.** If steps 1–2 don't surface enough depth, **STOP and emit the hardened brand-first prompt** in `references/case-study-research-prompt.md`. It demands a named source per claim, forces the scaling mechanism to step-by-step level, requires founder-primary material, and confines India to one short closing section (the brand sections interrogate founder, product, consumer insight, positioning, reason-to-win, and the scaling engine; India is one short closing section). Fill `{BRAND}`, `{CATEGORY}`, `{INDIA_ANGLE}`, emit it in a copy-paste code block, and stop. Always include the escape hatch: "if you want me to draft now with current knowledge and `{placeholder}` markers, say 'draft now' — but the scorecard will flag the gaps."
4. **Never fabricate.** Unverifiable number → `{placeholder}` + flag in the tail.

**Don't disappear into research.** 2–3 searches is the ceiling before drafting or escalating to deep research.

#### Grade before drafting

When the user returns with the report (or pasted a rich brief), **do not draft yet.** Grade it against `references/dossier-scorecard.md`. It is a hard gate, not a vibe check. The three blocking items: every load-bearing claim has a named source with at least one primary; the scaling engine is explained at mechanism level; the founder is a specific human with a real origin moment. The three flag items: single-source risk, the defining decision named and dated, India kept to a side note.

- **All three blocking items pass → resume at Step 4.** Note any flags for the provenance block.
- **Any blocking item fails → emit a targeted top-up prompt for only the missing pieces** (not a full re-run) and stop. Do not draft on a failed scorecard, and do not paper over a load-bearing gap with `{placeholder}`.

If the user said "draft now" over a failing scorecard, draft but make the gaps explicit in the provenance block and the tail — never silently.

A passing scorecard means the research is *deep*, not *accurate*. Accuracy is a separate gate: run `piyush-fact-check` on the finished draft before it reaches Piyush. Depth in, wrong facts out.

### Step 4 — Draft using the case-study skeleton

Open `references/case-study-template.md` and follow the skeleton. **You must read it** — it encodes the brand-to-India weighting and the load-bearing structural moves.

While drafting:
- Use one opening hook pattern (see Hooks below). Don't mix two.
- Hold the brand as the subject through ~70% of the post.
- Pivot to India in a single clean turn, late.
- Keep the India section to 1–3 short paragraphs naming 2–3 real Indian founders/brands.
- Close on an aphoristic compression that reframes the brand's lesson as the bet Piyush is making in India.

#### Opening hooks (pick one)

- **Mystery-noun hook (preferred, July 2026)** — a short titular line that describes the brand without naming it, making the reader earn the name. *"The brand four thousand teenagers built."* Piyush approved this pattern whole on the Bubble case study. Prefer it over a fact-led opener that gives the name and the number away in line one ("Bubble Skincare had a 4,600-person advisory group" is the same information with the tension removed).
- **Banned: the resume opener.** Never open with founder credentials (degrees, prior titles, prior companies). Open with a scene or moment where the founder does or realises something. Credentials, if they matter, arrive later as one clause. A rejected exemplar: "She began her degree at 15 and by 21 was running the consumer arm of a London-listed company."

- **Aphoristic statement** — short declarative claim, often a definition reversal. *"Heritage is not the obstacle to health. It is the moat."*
- **Paradox / counter-intuitive fact** — *"In January 2025, PepsiCo paid $1.2 billion for an eleven-year-old brand a family started in a Laredo kitchen."*
- **Dated scene** — time + place + specific detail containing the brand.
- **Claim** — provocative thesis sentence, 10–20 words.

#### Closing moves (default to the first)

- **Aphoristic compression** (his default) — one short line compressing the argument, echoing the opener. *"Heritage is not the constraint. It is the unfair advantage."*
- **Twist-and-echo** — echo the opening line with a reframe.
- **Cause-effect restate** — *"It works because…"*

### Step 5 — Strip AI tells

Scan and fix before output:

| Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Em-dashes (—) used at all | He used 0 in 2026. Use commas, periods, line breaks. Zero is the target. |
| "It's not just X — it's Y" closer | Rewrite as *"X is not Y. It is Z."* or cut. |
| India takes over the body | Hold the brand as subject through ~70%. Cut the India section back if it exceeds ~35% of word count. |
| Thin brand (founder = one clause, no product/insight/scaling detail) | The research was pointed at India. Go back to Step 3, demand the six brand-sections. |
| "Thrilled to announce" / "Excited to" | Not a deal post. Cut. Lead with the brand. |
| "In today's fast-paced world" / "At the end of the day" / "Game-changer" / "Paradigm shift" / "leverage" (verb) / "ecosystem" (overused) | Cut entirely. |
| Stacked emoji bullets, rocket emojis, unicode bold | Strip. Use a single-line paragraph for emphasis. |
| Slash-numbered lists (1/, 2/, 3/) | Use prose or `>>` arrow subheads. |
| "Hot take" / "Unpopular opinion" / "Let that sink in" | Cut. He observes; he doesn't perform. |
| Generic "What do you think? Drop your thoughts below" | Default to aphoristic compression close. |
| Five+ generic hashtags | Cut to 0–3 specific tags (#ConsumerBrands, #PrathVentures + 1 category-specific). |
| Adjective stacks ("genuinely impressive, deeply thoughtful, world-class") | Cut to one adjective or none. |
| Hedging stacks ("perhaps", "arguably", "might be considered") | State the claim. If it doesn't hold without the hedge, cut it. |
| Attribution stacks as register ("As she has said…", "reportedly", "roughly" recurring through the post) | Verify in the gate, then write declaratively. Hedges live in the provenance block, not the prose. One "estimated" on a genuinely soft figure max. |
| Analyst-deck vocabulary ("channel fit", "customer participation", "aesthetic differentiation", "optimize for", "strategic options", banker names) | Cut or translate into plain observation. Piyush is an investor speaking, not a consulting memo. Rejected exemplar: "Bubble optimized for customer participation and channel fit." |
| Manufactured turns ("And then…", "But here's the thing…", "Plot twist:") | Cut the signpost; let the next sentence carry the turn. |
| Recap conclusions ("So in summary…", "To wrap up…") | End on compression, not summary. |

### Step 6 — Self-check rubric

Any "no" = revise.

1. Does the opener match a hook pattern and contain (or immediately set up) the brand?
2. Is the brand the subject through ~70% of the post? Is the India section ~35% or less?
3. Does the body answer "why did *this brand* win" in concrete, dated, brand-specific detail?
4. Founder is a named human with a specific origin moment (not "two brothers")?
5. Brand numbers (revenue arc, raise, exit price) real and dated?
6. At least one Indian proper noun in the opening ~100 words; 2–3 real Indian founders/brands named in the India lesson?
7. Recency: was the soft flag applied if the brand's moment is 5+ years back?
8. Median paragraph ≤ 25 words; at least 2 single-line beats; at least one tricolon?
9. Zero em-dashes? No banned phrases?
10. Close is an aphoristic compression (or twist-and-echo) that reframes the brand's lesson for India?
11. Word count 350–480?
12. Does this read like an investor who studied the brand, or like a content machine? If the latter — soften, lower volume, add brand-specific detail.
13. Is there a character-revealing moment (the brand paying a cost to act on its values)? If research surfaced none, is that flagged in the tail?
14. At least three machine-room specifics (team composition, iteration counts, process mechanics)?
15. Is the register declarative — no attribution stacks, no analyst-deck vocabulary, hedges confined to the provenance block?
16. If a personal-stake line is used, is it sourced to the personal-details bank or a project file, or flagged "PERSONAL CLAIM — confirm true before publishing" in the tail?

### Step 7 — Output

Format:

```
[The post body, ready to paste into LinkedIn. No headers, no markdown,
no commentary. Hashtags at the end on their own line if applicable.]

---

**Post type:** Global D2C Case Study
**Brand:** [name] | **Defining moment:** [year]
**Word count:** [N] | **Brand-to-India split:** [~X% brand / Y% India]

**Research provenance:**
- Depth: [N primary sources, M secondary]. Scorecard: [pass / pass with flags].
- Spine claim: [the load-bearing insight] — source: [primary source]
- Scaling engine: sourced to [founder interview / filing / named analysis]
- Exit / scale: [figure] — source: [link]
- Single-source flags: [any load-bearing claim resting on one source — how handled]

**Flagged for review:**
- [Recency flag if applicable — and Akshay's confirmation status]
- [Placeholders / numbers / founder spellings to verify]
- [Any deliberate voice-guide deviation, e.g., "skipped team tag per your instruction"]
- [Lines least sure about voice-wise]
```

The research-provenance block is five lines Piyush can scan to know the work was done. It is the fix for him re-verifying everything himself. If he wants to dig, the sources are right there; the default is he trusts it and edits voice, not facts.

If nothing to flag, write "Nothing flagged — clean draft." Don't manufacture concerns.

## Important behaviours

### Don't over-write
Target 350–480 words. AI defaults toward 500+. If over 480, cut — usually from restated points, setup paragraphs that delay the thesis, or adjective stacks.

### Hashtags and team tags are an Akshay decision
If the user signalled hashtags/team tags off, follow the override and note it in the tail. Otherwise default to a light tag set for category posts (#ConsumerBrands #PrathVentures + 1 category-specific); no team tag unless asked.

### Disclosure
If Prath is invested in an Indian brand named in the India lesson, the disclosure line ("Disclosure: Prath Ventures is an investor in [brand].") goes in the India section. This is mandatory when Prath is invested.

### Implicit narratives stay implicit
Posts often carry an LP-signal or category-bet subtext. Build it into structure (which brand is chosen, which Indian founders get named) — never write "this is a signal to LPs".

### When the user pastes a draft to fix
Treat their draft as thesis intent, not voice template. Rewrite in Piyush's voice using this skeleton. Preserve their specific numbers, founder names, portfolio context, and explicit constraints. Don't preserve sentence structure or a hook that doesn't fit.

## Reference files

- **`references/case-study-template.md`** — The full skeleton for the case-study post type, with the brand-to-India weighting, theme palette, length target, the Indianness rule for this type, and an annotated exemplar. **Read this every time** before drafting.
- **`references/case-study-research-prompt.md`** — The hardened brand-first deep-research prompt (a named source per claim, the scaling mechanism forced to step-by-step level, founder-primary material, India as one short closing section), the recency-flag wording, and a targeted top-up prompt for when research is 80% there. Read this whenever the research gate triggers.
- **`references/dossier-scorecard.md`** — The depth gate the research must clear before drafting. Three blocking items (sourcing, mechanism, founder reality) and three flags. Read and apply this when research returns, before Step 4.

## Edges over polish — the final principle

When a draft feels too clean, too elegantly resolved, it is too AI. Piyush's posts have edges: a sentence that sounds slightly underbaked, a pivot that lands a beat early, a close that compresses without being clever. If your draft sounds like content, rewrite it until it sounds like an investor who studied a brand carefully and decided to say what he learned once. That is the bar.
