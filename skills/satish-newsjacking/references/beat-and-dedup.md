# Beat, dedup, and discipline (Phase 2)

## Satish's beat

Satish reacts to news at the intersection of these. A candidate is on-beat if it touches one of them with a real event, deal, filing, policy move, or number:

- ESOP buybacks, tender offers, employee liquidity events (India startups)
- Secondary transactions and secondary funds (India and global private markets)
- Equity / RSU / comp-policy changes, especially legacy finance vs fintech for talent
- AI and fintech talent wars where equity, retention, vesting, or big offers are the story
- Regulatory / tax moves touching ESOPs, secondaries, cross-border equity (SEBI, RBI, ED, Income Tax, MCA)
- IPO / DRHP filings where ESOP wealth, lock-ins, or pre-IPO liquidity is a sub-plot
- Layoffs, down-rounds, shutdowns where the angle is what happens to employee equity

He posts as an operator, not an influencer: observational, grounded, contrarian when the facts support it, never preachy.

## The dedup status system

For every candidate, reconcile against the actual post archive first (`satish-hissa-linkedin-posts.md`, `satish-hissa-linkedin-posts-51-100.md`) via `project_knowledge_search`. Search by company name AND by angle. Then assign:

- **OPEN ✅** — zero logged coverage of this company or this angle. Clean to use.
- **CONTESTED ⚠️** — the company is new but the angle overlaps something posted recently, or the company appeared recently but on a different angle. Usable only if a genuinely different angle exists. Always name the overlapping prior post and its date so Akshay can judge.
- **COVERED ❌** — he posted this company or this exact angle recently, or it merely restates a standing thesis. Skip.

A company name appearing in a past post does NOT automatically mean COVERED — check what angle that post took. Example: Zepto can appear in a quick-commerce unit-economics post while its DRHP/secondary/ESOP story stays completely OPEN.

## The standing-thesis trap

These are positions Satish returns to across his feed. A candidate that only restates one of these is COVERED even if the company is new. Look past the company to the underlying claim:

1. Secondaries are quietly becoming the most consistent way employees see money, more than IPOs.
2. The old three-exit model (buyback, IPO, M&A) is breaking; secondaries are the emerging fourth pillar.
3. An IPO is the most visible but not the most common way employees realise ESOP value.
4. Partial sale (selling some, holding the rest) signals confidence; worry when insiders sell all.
5. Large ESOP expense / stock-based comp as a share of revenue is the clearest signal of real employee ownership.
6. Legacy finance can match RSU numbers on a spreadsheet but can't manufacture the feeling of building something from zero.
7. Post-IPO, the question shifts from "when do we get liquidity" to "how do we time it" — sell a little, hold a little.
8. ESOPs are a wealth-creation tool, not a retention tool; liquidity is what makes them real.
9. Quiet, capital-efficient liquidity (a small raise that ends in a real cash exit) deserves more attention than loud headline offers.

If the candidate's only available angle is one of these, mark it COVERED and say which thesis it repeats.

## Factual discipline

- Numbers from the research prompts come off X reposts or aggregators. In Phase 3 they must be verified against a primary source (the filing, the company statement) before they enter the prep sheet body or any thumbnail. Flag whatever can't be verified.
- Distinguish directly-sourced claims from inference. Never silently resolve an unverified item — flag it.
- An X-only claim with no primary source is UNVERIFIED, even if it sounds plausible and is getting traction.
- Public financial figures a guest or founder cited get flagged for confirmation before use on thumbnails or posts.

## Sensitive-topic handling

Some on-beat stories involve a regulator, an investigation, a summons, a named individual's conduct, or unproven allegations (ED/FEMA actions, fraud probes, founder disputes). These are usable but require care:

- Never imply guilt or wrongdoing about a named, listing-bound company or person.
- Write the safe framing into the prep sheet: state exactly what is on record (e.g. "founders were summoned, complied, and the filing records no finding of wrongdoing"). Nothing beyond the record.
- In the prep sheet, give this thread the amber `flagged` card treatment and spell out the safe framing so Satish doesn't have to reconstruct it.

## Content and voice constraints (for the prep sheet and any future post)

The prep sheet itself is internal prep, but these rules shape what gets surfaced and how the "what to weigh" section is written:

- Operator voice, grounded and observational. No preachiness, no hype.
- No em-dashes in prose; use spaced hyphens. No triadic fragment lists. No AI tells ("but here's the thing", symmetric flourishes).
- The prep sheet never writes Satish's take. It surfaces the herd read and the operator counter-read and the open questions, and stops there.
- Operational reminders to carry in the footer: podcast links go in comments not the post body; text-only posts outperform quote cards at his follower count (no quote cards); any figure on a thumbnail must be verified first.
- Posts that would reference Verismo, Revvx, or YourNest need Satish's direct sign-off — do not surface those as angles without flagging that gate.
