## Currency preference

## Structure (3 parts, in this order)

The defining feature of an un# intro is that **it doesn't open with the guest**. It opens with a **macro context line** that frames why this conversation matters right now. The guest enters second.

### 1. Macro context — the "why now"
Open with a sentence (or two) that frames the market, regulatory, or technological moment this conversation sits inside. This is what makes the LinkedIn scroller stop. Concrete numbers and named players make it real.

The context should be one of:
- **Market shift** — a number that signals where capital is moving. ("Bitcoin ETFs now hold over $130B in assets." / "Stablecoins settled $27 trillion in transfers in 2024, more than Visa and Mastercard combined.")
- **Regulatory moment** — a policy or framework shift. ("The EU's MiCA framework comes into full force this year, forcing every European crypto firm to choose a side.")
- **Technical inflection** — a real capability that just became possible. ("Layer 2 fees are now under a cent, finally making on-chain consumer apps viable.")
- **Convergence moment** — TradFi and DeFi colliding. ("BlackRock, Franklin Templeton, and Fidelity are now all tokenizing money market funds on Ethereum.")

The context must be **factual and time-current**. Pull the number from the dossier or transcript. Don't invent.

### 2. Guest intro — short and credibility-loaded
One or two sentences. Name, company, what the company does in one line that lands for a crypto-literate but not protocol-expert audience.

Pattern: `[Name] is the founder of [Company], which [does X for Y audience].`

If the guest has a notable second-line credibility (prior exit, ex-Goldman, prior protocol they built, regulator background), include it as a clause. One credibility line, not three.

Examples:
- "Matthew Hougan is the CIO of Bitwise, the asset manager that filed the first US Bitcoin ETF before BlackRock did."
- "Marc Boiron runs Polygon Labs, the team behind the most-used Ethereum scaling network with $1B+ in TVL across its rollups."
- "Edul Patel founded Mudrex, the crypto investment platform with $50M+ ARR serving Indian retail investors."

### 3. Episode tease — the "In this episode..." line
End with a specific tease tied to a real moment, claim, or mechanic from the transcript.

**Canonical podcast-name phrasing:** spoken aloud it is "the Unhashed podcast" (Sri says "Unhashed" on tape, never "un#"). Written, the brand is "un#" (lowercase "un", hash symbol); never write "un hashtag".

un# uses **two natural slots** for the podcast name, and the strongest intros use one or the other (sometimes both):

1. **Opening slot — guest introduction style.** Weave the podcast name into part 2 when introducing the guest: "Henry Zhang joins us on the Unhashed podcast, the founder of DigiFT..." or "Today on Unhashed, we have Steven Goldfeder, co-founder of Offchain Labs..."
2. **Tease slot — episode hook style.** End with "In this episode of Unhashed, expect a deep look at..." or "In this episode of the Unhashed podcast, Henry breaks down..."

Default to the **opening slot** for un#. It matches the show's conversational, guest-first feel and keeps the closing tease punchy without an extra clause. Use both slots only if the intro runs long enough to justify the repetition.

For un#, **the strongest tease angles are insight-driven, not narrative-driven**. The audience wants to understand the space better, not hear another founder story.

## Hook library — 5 tease angles

1. **Mental model / insight (default)** — a counterintuitive framing the guest articulated. ("In this episode, Marc explains why most Layer 2s are competing on the wrong axis, and what really drives developer migration.")
2. **Mechanic / how-it-works** — a specific mechanism the listener will leave understanding. ("In this episode, Henry breaks down how DigiFT tokenizes a Treasury bill, settles it on-chain, and stays compliant in Singapore.")
3. **Regulatory / policy reading** — what the guest sees happening in policy that the listener doesn't. ("In this episode, Amanda lays out the three regulatory shifts coming in 2025 that will reshape stablecoin issuance.")
4. **Market call / thesis** — a sharp, contestable view on where the space is going. ("In this episode, Matthew explains why he thinks the next bull market will look nothing like 2021, and which sectors will absorb the institutional capital.")
5. **Founder bet** — what the guest staked their career on. ("In this episode, Sebastien shares why he bet The Sandbox on user-generated worlds when every other metaverse went corporate.")
6. **Contrarian / what-everyone-gets-wrong** — the guest's argument against consensus. ("In this episode, Arthur explains why he thinks proof-of-stake security claims are overstated, and what Tezos does differently.")

Label each variation with its hook type (e.g., `Variation A (Mental model / insight)`).

## What's allowed un-unpacked

This audience handles these terms without explanation:
- DeFi, CeFi, TradFi
- Stablecoin, token, smart contract, wallet
- L1, L2, rollup (basic level)
- TVL, AUM
- Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, Polygon by name

These need a one-line unpack:
- Restaking, MEV, ZK proofs, RWA tokenization, intent-based architectures
- Names of less-known protocols (drop a clause: "Mudrex, the crypto investment platform...")
- Regulatory frameworks beyond US/India (MiCA, MAS, BMA) — name the country.

## Recommendation guidance

Default to the **mental model / insight hook**. The un# audience presses play because they want to understand the space better. They're already convinced crypto matters; they want sharper thinking about it.

Exception: when the guest is the central figure in a specific market or regulatory event (a CEO during a major exchange collapse, a regulator at a turning point), the contrarian or regulatory hook can land harder. But default to insight.

## Worked example

> "Stablecoins settled $27 trillion in transfers last year, more than Visa and Mastercard combined, but most of that volume sits in two issuers facing very different regulatory regimes. Henry Zhang joins us on the Unhashed podcast. He's the founder of DigiFT, the Singapore-licensed exchange tokenizing US Treasuries and money market funds on-chain. In this episode, Henry breaks down how a tokenized T-bill actually settles, why MAS approval changed the business overnight, and what TradFi institutions are quietly buying."

**Word count:** ~75 words.

### Annotation

- **Part 1 (context):** "$27 trillion in transfers... more than Visa and Mastercard combined" — a real, current number that frames the stakes. Names the implicit tension (two issuers, different regimes) without explaining it, because the audience can fill that in.
- **Part 2 (guest):** Name, company, one-line what-it-does ("Singapore-licensed exchange tokenizing US Treasuries"). One credibility line, no chained bio.
- **Part 3 (tease):** Three specific things: settlement mechanic, regulatory moment ("MAS approval"), and a contrarian hint ("TradFi institutions are quietly buying"). Each could only belong to this episode.

## Style notes specific to un#

- The audience is global, so don't lead with India-specific framings unless the guest is doing something India-specific that matters globally (e.g., Mudrex, Indian regulatory clarity).
- Sri's voice is curious and unpretentious. Avoid anything that sounds like a Bloomberg anchor ("In a watershed moment for the digital asset industry..."). Keep it grounded.
- Crypto numbers should be current. If you cite TVL, market cap, or ETF assets, pull from the transcript or dossier — these move fast.
- It's fine to use technical terms (rollup, AMM, oracle, validator) once, but if a niche term appears, give it a half-sentence unpack within the same line.

## Pre-interview mode

In pre-interview mode, only the dossier exists. Parts 1 (macro context) and 2 (guest intro) are unchanged — both come from the dossier and current market data, not the transcript. Only part 3 (tease) shifts.

**Part 3 — tease shifts to anticipatory framing.** Replace transcript-based verbs ("breaks down", "explains why", "shares how") with anticipation verbs ("we'll explore", "expect a conversation about", "I want to understand", "we're going deep on").

Topic territory in un# pre-interview mode draws from:
- The guest's named protocol / company and its specific mechanism
- Public regulatory or technical positions the guest has taken (Twitter posts, prior podcasts, papers)
- The macro moment the conversation sits inside (the context line in part 1)
- Convergence points between the guest's work and broader market shifts

### Worked example (pre-interview version)

> "Stablecoins settled $27 trillion in transfers last year, more than Visa and Mastercard combined, but most of that volume sits in two issuers facing very different regulatory regimes. Henry Zhang joins us on the Unhashed podcast. He's the founder of DigiFT, the Singapore-licensed exchange tokenizing US Treasuries and money market funds on-chain. In this conversation, expect a deep look at how tokenized T-bills actually settle on a regulated venue, what MAS licensing changes about the business model, and where Henry sees TradFi institutions placing their tokenized-asset bets in 2025."

**Word count:** ~85 words.

### Notes

- Parts 1 and 2 are identical to the post-recording version — they're dossier-based.
- The tease names three specific topics (settlement mechanics, MAS licensing, TradFi adoption) that are all visible in the dossier. None require a transcript.
- "Expect a deep look at" replaces "Henry breaks down" — same specificity, different temporal frame.
