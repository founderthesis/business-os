# Proposal: ft-lower-thirds (editing doc v2, decision 602)

Target skill: ft-lower-thirds

Why: Sarika rebuilt the lower-thirds tab by hand on Mandar's episode. She restored every term the skill had pruned (TAM, CAC, IRR, power law, binary bet, SIP, pre-emptive right) and kept only 3 data visuals out of 15-plus. Her ask in the editing chat, 16 Nov: "identify any specialized terminology, acronyms or jargons", "increase the number of lower thirds vs brolls and motion graphics or data viz". Decision 602 (Akshay, 17 Nov): every technical term with its timestamp, no picking, spacing or timing; at most 3 data visualisations, only where one can become a short or clip.

## Change 1: description (frontmatter)
Before:
> Produces a timestamped table the editor can work straight from, with a second pass that removes anything the audience already knows.

After:
> Produces a complete timestamped table of every technical term, brand and named person, for the editor to choose from, plus at most 3 data visualisations that can stand alone as a short or clip.

## Change 2: the audience paragraph
Before:
> **Assume a mature audience that already knows basic business terminology.** Explaining "EBITDA" or "Series A" is condescending and wastes screen time. Explain the specialised thing: the industry term, the regulatory acronym, the technology, the named person a general audience would not recognise.

After:
> **Assume a mature audience that knows basic business words** (revenue, profit, startup, investor, funding). Leave only those out. Every other technical term, acronym, finance or industry term, insider phrase, brand, company, fund, product and named person gets a row. The editor picks what goes on screen; this skill's job is a complete list.

## Change 3: output format
Before: table `Timestamp | Term / phrase | Lower third text or B-roll`, three kinds of entry (Definitions, Visuals, Data representations), with "whenever a number, a comparison, or a trend is discussed, recommend how to show it."

After: same three columns (Sarika's house format). Each cell starts with a type word:
- `LT:` one-line plain-English definition, under 15 words
- `Visual:` logo or photo for a brand, company, fund, product or person
- `VIZ:` a data visualisation. At most 3 per episode, only where the data moment can stand alone as a short or clip. Name that short or clip in the cell. No other data, text or motion graphics.

Mark with † any row whose timestamp falls inside a cut proposed in the cut list.

## Change 4: delete "Second pass" and "Keep the first 90 seconds clear"
Before: two sections telling the skill to cut known terms, terms the guest explains, entries within a few seconds of each other ("A tight list of 15 the editor uses beats 40 they ignore"), and to hold back lower thirds in the first 90 seconds.

After: both removed. Replace with:
> ## Completeness check
> Before you hand over, scan the transcript once more for acronyms (any run of capitals), numbers with units, and proper nouns. Every one that is not a basic business word must have a row at its first use. Expect well over 50 rows for a long episode (Mandar, 106 min raw: 151). Spacing, timing and the first-90-seconds call are the editor's, not this skill's.

## Unchanged
