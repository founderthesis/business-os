# Floris Jansen, Vectify: from Apple store manager at 21 to cold email at scale

Floris Jansen ran two Apple stores before he was 22, then failed at his first software company. Today his six-person team runs cold email and calling for US and European businesses, about 4.5 million touches a month.

## Cold outreach, done for you or by you

Vectify runs outbound for other companies. Its core is cold email, with phone calls alongside, and most of its clients are in the US, with some in Europe.

The team does about four and a half million emails and calls a month across its customers. The first job is pipeline: getting meetings booked. With the customers it works most closely with, the goal moves further down the funnel, to revenue it can help scale.

There are two kinds of customer. A few are large US enterprises. The rest are smaller companies that want to run cold outreach themselves without building the plumbing.

## Apple at 21, then a startup he hated

Jansen went to work for Apple straight after high school and stayed four years. By 21 he was the manager of two stores, with 45 employees and 12.5 million in revenue between them.

He then joined a startup and, in his words, "hated my job." He used the time to learn to code, and started his first software company. It "massively failed."

In the process he found out he was very good at booking meetings. He started doing it for a first few companies, and that work turned into Vectify. The company is now three years old.

## Service or software? Both, on purpose

Ask Jansen whether Vectify is a platform or an agency and he'll grant the point. It "comes super close to a service," he says.

The product side is real. Customers can log in, onboard themselves and set up their own campaigns. The system finds leads, sets up the sending mailboxes and handles most of the settings. The customer still writes the copy.

What sits on top is people. Each customer gets one assigned engineer and always talks to the same person, so that engineer learns what the customer is after and tunes the campaigns. Jansen calls the result a combination of service and software.

He also splits the business by customer size. Large companies get full onboarding and a done-for-you service. Smaller ones, for whom a big retainer doesn't work, pay less and do most of the work on the platform themselves, with an engineer on call.

## How the model works

For large clients, Vectify starts by testing what to say. The team cold calls the leads to learn which offer lands, then turns that offer into cold email and runs it at volume. Customers that do well get scaled up, with outreach to their entire addressable market every 60 to 90 days.

The self-service tier starts at $600 a month. The pitch against cheaper tools is consolidation. A typical outbound stack has four parts: a lead database such as Apollo, an enrichment and personalization tool such as Clay, a sequencer such as Instantly, and a provider for the sending inboxes. Vectify puts all four in one place. It uses the same kinds of providers underneath; the difference is that the customer doesn't have to stitch them together or learn how each one works.

Jansen says inbox costs are part of the argument too. Bought separately at the same sending volume, he puts the inboxes alone at an extra $100 to $200 a month or more, on top of the sequencer.

## What the numbers say

The figures Jansen shared on the call are operating ones, not financial ones:

- A team of six, three years in.
- About 4.5 million emails and phone calls a month.
- Clients mostly in the US, some in Europe, and some in the Middle East.
- Self-service pricing from $600 a month.

## What's next

Jansen wants more visibility in Europe and the US, where his customers are. He also looks further afield for people: the company has had a lot of talent from India, including Indians living in Europe.

He's clear about what makes the story worth telling. "The product is one thing, the founder story is something else."
